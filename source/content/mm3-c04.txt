## 4.2 | The second derivative
@p The **second derivative** is the derivative of the derivative: $f''(x)=\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$. It is the **rate of change of the gradient**. If $f''(x)>0$ the gradient is increasing (the curve bends upward, **concave up**); if $f''(x)<0$ the gradient is decreasing (**concave down**).
@p In kinematics, if $x$ is position then $x'$ is velocity and $x''$ is acceleration.
@ex Second derivative
Q: Find $f''(x)$ for $f(x)=x^3e^{-x}$.
S: $f'(x)=3x^2e^{-x}-x^3e^{-x}=e^{-x}(3x^2-x^3)$.
S: $f''(x)=-e^{-x}(3x^2-x^3)+e^{-x}(6x-3x^2)=e^{-x}(x^3-6x^2+6x)$.
A: $f''(x)=e^{-x}(x^3-6x^2+6x)$

## 4.3 | Concavity and points of inflection
@key **Stationary point test.** At $f'(a)=0$: if $f''(a)>0$ it is a **local minimum**; if $f''(a)<0$ a **local maximum**; if $f''(a)=0$ the test fails, so check the sign of $f'$ either side.
@key A **point of inflection** is where concavity changes: $f''(x)=0$ **and** $f''$ changes sign. A **stationary** point of inflection also has $f'(x)=0$.
@ex Classifying stationary points
Q: Classify the stationary points of $f(x)=x^3-3x^2$.
S: $f'(x)=3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)$, so $x=0$ or $x=2$.
S: $f''(x)=6x-6$. At $x=0$: $-6<0$, max, point $(0,0)$. At $x=2$: $6>0$, min, point $(2,-4)$.
A: Max at $(0,0)$, min at $(2,-4)$.
@ex Point of inflection
Q: Find the point of inflection of $f(x)=x^3-3x^2$.
S: $f''(x)=6x-6=0\Rightarrow x=1$.
S: $f''$ is negative for $x<1$ and positive for $x>1$, so concavity changes. $f(1)=-2$.
A: $(1,-2)$
@warn $f''(a)=0$ does not guarantee an inflection point. Compare $y=x^4$: $f''(0)=0$ but $x=0$ is a minimum and concavity never changes. Always check the sign change.

## 4.4 | Curve sketching
@p Checklist for sketching $y=f(x)$: (1) intercepts ($y$-int at $x=0$; $x$-int at $y=0$); (2) stationary points from $f'=0$ and their nature; (3) inflection points from $f''=0$; (4) asymptotes and end behaviour; (5) join smoothly, respecting concavity.
@ex Sketching
Q: Sketch $f(x)=xe^{-x}$ for $x\ge0$ by finding its key features.
S: Intercept: $(0,0)$. $f'(x)=e^{-x}(1-x)=0\Rightarrow x=1$, $f(1)=e^{-1}$.
S: $f''(x)=e^{-x}(x-2)$. At $x=1$: $-e^{-1}<0$, so a local max at $(1,e^{-1})$.
S: $f''=0$ at $x=2$ with a sign change: inflection at $\left(2,2e^{-2}\right)$.
S: As $x\to\infty$, $f\to0$, so the $x$-axis is a horizontal asymptote.
A: Rises to a max $(1,e^{-1})$, inflects at $(2,2e^{-2})$, then decays to $0$.

## 4.5 | Optimisation
@p Optimisation problems ask for the greatest or least value of something. **Method:** (1) draw and define variables; (2) write the quantity to optimise in terms of **one** variable using the constraint; (3) state the domain; (4) differentiate and set to zero; (5) confirm max or min with $f''$ or a sign test, and check the **endpoints** of the domain; (6) answer in context with units.
@ex Open box
Q: A square-based open box has volume $32\ \text{cm}^3$. Find the dimensions that minimise surface area.
S: Base side $x$, height $h$: $x^2h=32\Rightarrow h=\frac{32}{x^2}$. Area $A=x^2+4xh=x^2+\frac{128}{x}$, $x>0$.
S: $A'=2x-\frac{128}{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x^3=64\Rightarrow x=4$.
S: $A''=2+\frac{256}{x^3}>0$, so a minimum. $h=\frac{32}{16}=2$.
A: Base $4\text{ cm}\times4\text{ cm}$, height $2$ cm; area $48\text{ cm}^2$.
@warn Finding $x$ is rarely the final answer. Re-read the question: it may want the minimum area, the dimension, or a time.
