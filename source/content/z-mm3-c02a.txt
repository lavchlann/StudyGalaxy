## 2.2
@p **What this topic is about.** Calculus with trigonometric functions only works in **radians**, and exact values from the unit circle appear in nearly every trig question in the technology-free paper. This topic rebuilds radian measure, the unit circle, exact values, the quadrant signs, symmetry identities, and arc length and sector area, so that later differentiation and modelling are smooth.
@key **Radians.** One radian is the angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius. A full turn is $2\pi$ radians, so $\pi\text{ rad}=180^\circ$. Degrees to radians: multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$. Radians to degrees: multiply by $\frac{180}{\pi}$.
@p **Why radians?** In radians the arc length is simply $s=r\theta$, and (as you will see in 2.4) $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x$ exactly. In degrees every calculus formula picks up an awkward factor of $\frac{\pi}{180}$. So in this unit, assume radians unless a degree symbol is shown, and set your calculator to radian mode.
@key **The unit circle.** For the point $P$ on a circle of radius 1 at angle $\theta$ (measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis): $P=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. Pythagoras on the unit circle gives the identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.
@p **Exact values from two triangles.** Half an equilateral triangle of side 2 has sides $1,\sqrt3,2$ and angles $\frac\pi6$ and $\frac\pi3$. Half a unit square has sides $1,1,\sqrt2$ and angle $\frac\pi4$. Reading SOH-CAH-TOA from these gives: $\sin\frac\pi6=\frac12$, $\cos\frac\pi6=\frac{\sqrt3}2$, $\tan\frac\pi6=\frac1{\sqrt3}$; $\sin\frac\pi4=\cos\frac\pi4=\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $\tan\frac\pi4=1$; $\sin\frac\pi3=\frac{\sqrt3}2$, $\cos\frac\pi3=\frac12$, $\tan\frac\pi3=\sqrt3$. Also $\sin0=0$, $\cos0=1$, $\sin\frac\pi2=1$, $\cos\frac\pi2=0$.
@p **Signs in each quadrant (ASTC).** Quadrant 1 ($0$ to $\frac\pi2$): **A**ll positive. Quadrant 2 ($\frac\pi2$ to $\pi$): only **S**in positive. Quadrant 3 ($\pi$ to $\frac{3\pi}2$): only **T**an positive. Quadrant 4 ($\frac{3\pi}{2}$ to $2\pi$): only **C**os positive. This follows from the signs of the $x$ and $y$ coordinates.
@p **Method for any exact value.** (1) Locate the angle's quadrant. (2) Find the **reference angle**, the acute angle to the $x$-axis: in Q2 it is $\pi-\theta$, in Q3 it is $\theta-\pi$, in Q4 it is $2\pi-\theta$. (3) Write the value for the reference angle. (4) Attach the sign from ASTC. Negative angles go clockwise; angles larger than $2\pi$ wrap around (subtract $2\pi$).
@ex Exact values outside the first quadrant (full working)
Q: Find the exact values of $\sin\frac{5\pi}{6}$, $\cos\frac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\tan\frac{5\pi}{3}$.
S: **$\frac{5\pi}6$:** this is between $\frac\pi2$ and $\pi$, so it is in quadrant 2. Reference angle $\pi-\frac{5\pi}6=\frac\pi6$. Sine is positive in Q2, so $\sin\frac{5\pi}6=+\sin\frac\pi6=\frac12$.
S: **$\frac{7\pi}6$:** this is between $\pi$ and $\frac{3\pi}2$, quadrant 3. Reference angle $\frac{7\pi}6-\pi=\frac\pi6$. Cosine is negative in Q3, so $\cos\frac{7\pi}6=-\cos\frac\pi6=-\frac{\sqrt3}2$.
S: **$\frac{5\pi}3$:** between $\frac{3\pi}2$ and $2\pi$, quadrant 4. Reference angle $2\pi-\frac{5\pi}3=\frac\pi3$. Tangent is negative in Q4, so $\tan\frac{5\pi}3=-\tan\frac\pi3=-\sqrt3$.
S: **Summary:** each time, find the quadrant, the reference angle, then the sign.
A: $\tfrac12$, $-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, $-\sqrt3$
@ex Using the Pythagorean identity (full working)
Q: Given $\sin\theta=\frac35$ and $\frac\pi2<\theta<\pi$, find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.
S: **Use $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$:** $\cos^2\theta=1-\left(\frac35\right)^2=1-\frac9{25}=\frac{16}{25}$.
S: **Take square roots:** $\cos\theta=\pm\frac45$.
S: **Choose the sign from the quadrant:** $\theta$ is in quadrant 2, where cosine is negative, so $\cos\theta=-\frac45$.
S: **Find tan:** $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\dfrac{3/5}{-4/5}=-\dfrac34$.
A: $\cos\theta=-\frac45$, $\tan\theta=-\frac34$
@key **Symmetry identities.** $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$, $\cos(\pi-\theta)=-\cos\theta$; $\sin(\pi+\theta)=-\sin\theta$, $\cos(\pi+\theta)=-\cos\theta$; $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$, $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$; $\sin\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\cos\theta$, $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\sin\theta$.
@p **Arc length and sector area.** For angle $\theta$ in radians in a circle of radius $r$: arc length $s=r\theta$ and sector area $A=\frac12r^2\theta$. Both come from taking the fraction $\frac{\theta}{2\pi}$ of the full circumference $2\pi r$ or area $\pi r^2$.
@ex Sector (full working)
Q: A sector has radius 6 cm and angle $\frac{2\pi}{3}$. Find the arc length and the area exactly, then the perimeter of the sector.
S: **Arc length:** $s=r\theta=6\times\frac{2\pi}3=4\pi$ cm.
S: **Area:** $A=\frac12r^2\theta=\frac12\times36\times\frac{2\pi}3=12\pi$ cm$^2$.
S: **Perimeter:** the boundary is the arc plus two radii: $4\pi+6+6=4\pi+12$ cm.
S: **Approximations:** $s\approx12.57$ cm, $A\approx37.70$ cm$^2$, perimeter $\approx24.57$ cm.
A: $s=4\pi$ cm, $A=12\pi$ cm², perimeter $4\pi+12$ cm
@ex Converting units (full working)
Q: Convert $225^\circ$ to radians and $\frac{7\pi}{12}$ to degrees.
S: **Degrees to radians:** $225\times\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{225\pi}{180}$.
S: **Simplify the fraction:** divide top and bottom by 45: $\frac{5\pi}{4}$.
S: **Radians to degrees:** $\frac{7\pi}{12}\times\frac{180}{\pi}=\frac{7\times180}{12}$.
S: **Simplify:** $\frac{1260}{12}=105^\circ$.
A: $\frac{5\pi}{4}$ and $105^\circ$
@warn Calculator in the wrong mode is the most common lost mark in trig. If the question uses $\pi$ or has no degree symbol, you must be in **radians**. Also, the reference angle is always measured to the **$x$-axis**, never the $y$-axis.
@p **Exam tip.** Draw a quick unit circle sketch with the angle marked. It takes ten seconds and prevents sign errors, and examiners can award method marks from it.
@check Exact value | Find the exact value of $\cos\frac{3\pi}{4}$.
A: -sqrt2/2
ALT: -√2/2 || -1/sqrt2 || -(sqrt2)/2 || -1/√2
AD: $-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$
SOL: $\frac{3\pi}4$ lies between $\frac\pi2$ and $\pi$, so it is in quadrant 2. || The reference angle is $\pi-\frac{3\pi}4=\frac\pi4$. || $\cos\frac\pi4=\frac{\sqrt2}2$. || Cosine is negative in quadrant 2, so $\cos\frac{3\pi}4=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$.
H: Which quadrant? || Reference angle $\pi-\theta$. || Cos is negative in Q2.
@check Pythagorean identity | If $\cos\theta=-\frac{5}{13}$ and $\pi<\theta<\frac{3\pi}{2}$, find $\sin\theta$.
A: -12/13
ALT: -(12/13)
AD: $-\tfrac{12}{13}$
SOL: Use $\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta=1-\frac{25}{169}=\frac{144}{169}$. || So $\sin\theta=\pm\frac{12}{13}$. || $\theta$ is in quadrant 3, where sine is negative. || Therefore $\sin\theta=-\frac{12}{13}$.
H: Use $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$. || Square root gives $\pm$. || Choose the sign for quadrant 3.
@check Radians | Convert $300^\circ$ to radians exactly.
A: 5pi/3
ALT: 5π/3 || (5pi)/3
AD: $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
SOL: Multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$: $300\times\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{300\pi}{180}$. || Divide top and bottom by 60. || This gives $\frac{5\pi}{3}$.
H: Multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$. || Simplify the fraction. || Divide by 60.
@check Sector area | Find the exact area of a sector with radius 4 and angle $\frac{5\pi}{8}$.
A: 5pi
ALT: 5π
AD: $5\pi$
SOL: Use $A=\frac12r^2\theta$. || Substitute: $A=\frac12\times16\times\frac{5\pi}{8}$. || Simplify: $8\times\frac{5\pi}{8}=5\pi$. || The area is $5\pi$ square units.
H: Sector area formula. || $r^2=16$. || $\frac12\times16=8$.
@check Symmetry | If $\sin\theta=0.4$, find $\sin(\pi+\theta)$ and $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)$.
A: -0.4,0.4
ALT: -0.4 and 0.4 || -2/5,2/5
AD: $-0.4$ and $0.4$
SOL: Adding $\pi$ moves the point to the opposite side of the circle, so both coordinates change sign: $\sin(\pi+\theta)=-\sin\theta=-0.4$. || The complementary-angle identity says $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\sin\theta$. || So $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=0.4$. || Answers: $-0.4$ and $0.4$.
H: What does adding $\pi$ do on the unit circle? || $\sin(\pi+\theta)=-\sin\theta$. || $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\sin\theta$.
## 2.3
@p **What this topic is about.** Sketching transformed sine, cosine and tangent graphs, reading their features, and solving trig equations over a given domain. Both skills feed directly into modelling (2.5), where a real situation becomes a sine or cosine equation you must solve.
@key For $y=a\sin\big(b(x-c)\big)+d$ (and similarly cosine): **amplitude** $|a|$; **period** $\frac{2\pi}{|b|}$; **horizontal shift** $c$ units right; **midline** $y=d$; **maximum** $d+|a|$; **minimum** $d-|a|$; **range** $[d-|a|,\ d+|a|]$. For $y=a\tan(b(x-c))+d$ the period is $\frac{\pi}{|b|}$ and there is no amplitude.
@p **Sketching in five key points.** Work out the period and split one period into four equal quarters, starting at $x=c$. A sine graph passes through: midline (start), maximum, midline, minimum, midline (end). A cosine graph passes through: maximum, midline, minimum, midline, maximum. If $a<0$, swap maxima and minima. Plot these five points, join with a smooth wave, then extend or trim to the required domain and label intercepts and endpoints.
@ex Reading features (full working)
Q: For $y=3\sin(2x)+1$, $0\le x\le2\pi$, state the amplitude, period, range and the coordinates of the first maximum.
S: **Amplitude:** $|a|=3$.
S: **Period:** $\frac{2\pi}{b}=\frac{2\pi}{2}=\pi$.
S: **Range:** midline $y=1$, so from $1-3=-2$ to $1+3=4$: $[-2,4]$.
S: **First maximum:** $\sin(2x)=1$ first when $2x=\frac\pi2$, i.e. $x=\frac\pi4$. Then $y=3(1)+1=4$.
S: **State:** the first maximum is $\left(\frac\pi4,4\right)$, and there are two full cycles on $[0,2\pi]$.
A: Amplitude 3, period $\pi$, range $[-2,4]$, first maximum $\left(\frac\pi4,4\right)$
@p **Solving trig equations: the routine.** (1) Rearrange to isolate the trig function, e.g. $\sin(\ldots)=k$. (2) If the argument is $bx$ or $b(x-c)$, **transform the domain** first: if $0\le x\le2\pi$ then $0\le2x\le4\pi$. (3) Find the reference angle from the exact value (or inverse trig of $|k|$). (4) Use ASTC to find every angle in the transformed domain with the right sign. (5) Convert back by dividing by $b$ (and adding $c$). (6) Check the count: $\sin$ and $\cos$ give two solutions per period.
@ex Equation with a doubled argument (full working)
Q: Solve $2\sin(2x)=\sqrt3$ for $0\le x\le\pi$.
S: **Isolate:** $\sin(2x)=\frac{\sqrt3}2$.
S: **Transform the domain:** if $0\le x\le\pi$ then $0\le2x\le2\pi$.
S: **Reference angle:** $\sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt3}2$ gives $\theta=\frac\pi3$.
S: **Quadrants:** sine is positive in quadrants 1 and 2, so $2x=\frac\pi3$ or $2x=\pi-\frac\pi3=\frac{2\pi}3$ (both inside $[0,2\pi]$).
S: **Divide by 2:** $x=\frac\pi6$ or $x=\frac\pi3$.
S: **Check:** $2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}3\right)=\sqrt3$ ✓ and $2\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}3\right)=\sqrt3$ ✓.
A: $x=\frac\pi6,\ \frac\pi3$
@ex Equation with a shift (full working)
Q: Solve $\sqrt2\cos\left(x-\frac\pi4\right)=1$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
S: **Isolate:** $\cos\left(x-\frac\pi4\right)=\frac1{\sqrt2}$.
S: **Transform the domain:** subtract $\frac\pi4$: $-\frac\pi4\le x-\frac\pi4\le\frac{7\pi}4$.
S: **Reference angle:** $\cos\theta=\frac1{\sqrt2}$ at $\theta=\frac\pi4$; cosine is positive in Q1 and Q4.
S: **All angles in $\left[-\frac\pi4,\frac{7\pi}4\right]$:** $-\frac\pi4$, $\frac\pi4$, $\frac{7\pi}4$.
S: **Add $\frac\pi4$:** $x=0,\ \frac\pi2,\ 2\pi$.
A: $x=0,\ \frac\pi2,\ 2\pi$
@ex Quadratic trig equation (full working)
Q: Solve $2\sin^2x-\sin x=0$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
S: **Factorise (do not divide by $\sin x$, which loses solutions):** $\sin x(2\sin x-1)=0$.
S: **Case 1:** $\sin x=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ \pi,\ 2\pi$.
S: **Case 2:** $\sin x=\frac12\Rightarrow$ reference angle $\frac\pi6$, positive in Q1 and Q2: $x=\frac\pi6,\ \frac{5\pi}6$.
S: **Combine in order:** $x=0,\frac\pi6,\frac{5\pi}6,\pi,2\pi$.
A: $x=0,\ \frac\pi6,\ \frac{5\pi}6,\ \pi,\ 2\pi$
@ex Rule from a graph (full working)
Q: A sinusoidal graph has a maximum of 5 at $x=\frac\pi6$, a minimum of $-1$, and period $\pi$. Write a cosine rule for it.
S: **Midline:** $d=\frac{\max+\min}2=\frac{5+(-1)}2=2$.
S: **Amplitude:** $a=\frac{\max-\min}2=\frac{6}2=3$.
S: **$b$ from the period:** $\frac{2\pi}{b}=\pi\Rightarrow b=2$.
S: **Shift:** a cosine graph starts at a maximum, and this one has its maximum at $x=\frac\pi6$, so shift right by $\frac\pi6$.
S: **Rule:** $y=3\cos\left(2\left(x-\frac\pi6\right)\right)+2$. Check: at $x=\frac\pi6$, $y=3\cos0+2=5$ ✓.
A: $y=3\cos\left(2\left(x-\frac\pi6\right)\right)+2$
@warn Transform the domain **before** solving. For $\sin(2x)$ on $0\le x\le\pi$ you must search $0\le2x\le2\pi$. Forgetting this loses half the solutions. Also inside a bracket, $\sin(2x-\frac\pi3)=\sin\left(2\left(x-\frac\pi6\right)\right)$: the shift is $\frac\pi6$, not $\frac\pi3$.
@p **Exam tip.** List all the solutions of the transformed angle first, then divide. Write them in ascending order, and state how many there are; markers check both.
@check Period and range | For $y=4\cos(3x)-2$, state the period and the range.
A: 2pi/3,[-6,2]
ALT: 2π/3,[-6,2] || 2pi/3 and [-6,2] || period 2pi/3, range [-6,2]
AD: Period $\frac{2\pi}{3}$; range $[-6,2]$
SOL: Period: $\frac{2\pi}{b}=\frac{2\pi}{3}$. || The amplitude is 4 and the midline is $y=-2$. || Maximum $-2+4=2$; minimum $-2-4=-6$. || So the range is $[-6,2]$.
H: Period is $\frac{2\pi}{b}$. || Midline is $y=d$. || Add and subtract the amplitude.
@check Solve on a domain | Solve $2\cos x=-1$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
A: 2pi/3,4pi/3
ALT: 2π/3,4π/3 || 2pi/3 and 4pi/3 || x=2pi/3,4pi/3
AD: $x=\frac{2\pi}{3},\ \frac{4\pi}{3}$
SOL: Isolate: $\cos x=-\frac12$. || Reference angle: $\cos\theta=\frac12$ at $\theta=\frac\pi3$. || Cosine is negative in quadrants 2 and 3: $x=\pi-\frac\pi3=\frac{2\pi}3$ and $x=\pi+\frac\pi3=\frac{4\pi}3$. || Both lie in $[0,2\pi]$.
H: Isolate $\cos x$. || Reference angle $\frac\pi3$. || Cosine negative in Q2 and Q3.
@check Doubled argument | Solve $\sin(2x)=\frac12$ for $0\le x\le\pi$.
A: pi/12,5pi/12
ALT: π/12,5π/12 || pi/12 and 5pi/12 || x=pi/12,5pi/12
AD: $x=\frac{\pi}{12},\ \frac{5\pi}{12}$
SOL: Transform the domain: $0\le2x\le2\pi$. || Reference angle $\frac\pi6$; sine positive in Q1 and Q2: $2x=\frac\pi6$ or $2x=\frac{5\pi}6$. || These are the only solutions in $[0,2\pi]$. || Divide by 2: $x=\frac\pi{12}$ or $x=\frac{5\pi}{12}$.
H: Change the domain to $0\le2x\le2\pi$. || $\sin\theta=\frac12$ at $\frac\pi6$ and $\frac{5\pi}6$. || Divide by 2.
@check Tan equation | Solve $\tan x=-1$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
A: 3pi/4,7pi/4
ALT: 3π/4,7π/4 || 3pi/4 and 7pi/4
AD: $x=\frac{3\pi}{4},\ \frac{7\pi}{4}$
SOL: Reference angle: $\tan\theta=1$ at $\theta=\frac\pi4$. || Tangent is negative in quadrants 2 and 4. || Q2: $\pi-\frac\pi4=\frac{3\pi}4$; Q4: $2\pi-\frac\pi4=\frac{7\pi}4$. || Solutions: $x=\frac{3\pi}4,\frac{7\pi}4$.
H: Reference angle for $\tan=1$. || Where is tan negative? || Use $\pi-\theta$ and $2\pi-\theta$.
@check Rule from features | A sine curve has maximum 9, minimum 1 and period $4\pi$. Find $a$, $b$ and $d$ in $y=a\sin(bx)+d$ (with $a,b>0$).
A: a=4,b=1/2,d=5
ALT: 4,1/2,5 || a=4, b=0.5, d=5 || 4,0.5,5
AD: $a=4,\ b=\frac12,\ d=5$
SOL: Midline: $d=\frac{9+1}2=5$. || Amplitude: $a=\frac{9-1}2=4$. || Period $\frac{2\pi}b=4\pi$, so $b=\frac{2\pi}{4\pi}=\frac12$. || So $y=4\sin\left(\frac x2\right)+5$.
H: Average of max and min. || Half the difference. || Solve $\frac{2\pi}{b}=4\pi$.
