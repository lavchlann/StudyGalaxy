## 3.2
@ex Chain rule, three layers
Q: Differentiate $y=\sin^3(2x)$.
S: Treat as $u^3$ with $u=\sin(2x)$: $3u^2\cdot u'$.
S: $u'=2\cos(2x)$.
A: $6\sin^2(2x)\cos(2x)$
@ex Chain rule with e
Q: Differentiate $y=e^{\sin x}$.
S: Outside $e^u$ gives $e^u$, inside $u=\sin x$ gives $\cos x$.
A: $\cos x\,e^{\sin x}$
@p **Why it works.** Rates multiply: if $y$ changes $\frac{dy}{du}$ times as fast as $u$, and $u$ changes $\frac{du}{dx}$ times as fast as $x$, then $y$ changes $\frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx}$ times as fast as $x$. Say the rule out loud: "derivative of the outside, leave the inside alone, times derivative of the inside".
## 3.3
@ex Product of three pieces
Q: Differentiate $y=x^2e^{x}\sin x$.
S: Differentiate one factor at a time, keeping the others: $2xe^x\sin x+x^2e^x\sin x+x^2e^x\cos x$.
A: $xe^x\left(2\sin x+x\sin x+x\cos x\right)$
@ex Product with chain
Q: Differentiate $y=x\,e^{-2x}$ and find its stationary point.
S: $y'=e^{-2x}-2xe^{-2x}=e^{-2x}(1-2x)$.
S: $e^{-2x}>0$, so $y'=0\Rightarrow x=\frac12$.
A: $y'=e^{-2x}(1-2x)$; stationary at $x=\frac12$.
## 3.4
@ex Quotient with logs
Q: Differentiate $y=\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ and find where it is stationary.
S: $y'=\dfrac{\frac1x\cdot x-\ln x\cdot1}{x^2}=\dfrac{1-\ln x}{x^2}$.
S: Zero when $\ln x=1$, so $x=e$.
A: $y'=\dfrac{1-\ln x}{x^2}$; stationary at $x=e$.
@warn Many quotient problems are easier as products: $\frac{e^x}{x^2}=e^xx^{-2}$. Choose whichever you trust under pressure.
## 3.5
@ex Normal line
Q: Find the normal to $y=e^{2x}$ at $x=0$.
S: $y'=2e^{2x}$, tangent gradient $2$; normal gradient $-\frac12$; point $(0,1)$.
A: $y=-\frac12x+1$
@ex Optimisation by differentiation
Q: A rectangle has perimeter $40$. Find the maximum area.
S: Sides $x$ and $20-x$, area $A=x(20-x)=20x-x^2$.
S: $A'=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10$; $A''=-2<0$ so a maximum.
A: $100$ square units (a square, $10\times10$).
## X | Exam toolkit
@p **Spotting the rule.** Sum or difference: differentiate each term. Product of two functions of $x$: product rule. Fraction with $x$ in the denominator: quotient rule (or a product with a negative power). Function inside a function: chain rule. Many questions need two of these at once.
@p **Verification habit.** After differentiating, test at one value. For $y=x e^{-2x}$ at $x=0$: $y'=e^0(1-0)=1$, and the curve visibly rises from the origin. A thirty second sanity check catches sign errors.

SOL: $2x\ln x+x^2\cdot\frac1x$.
H: Product rule.||$u=x^2$, $v=\ln x$.||Simplify $x^2\cdot\frac1x$.

@quiz Chain | Differentiate $y=\ln(5x-2)$.
A: 5/(5x-2)
AD: $\frac5{5x-2}$
SOL: $\frac{f'}{f}$.
H: $\frac{d}{dx}\ln f=\frac{f'}f$.||$f'=5$.||Write it.
@quiz Stationary | Where is $y=xe^{-x}$ stationary?
A: 1
ALT: x=1
AD: $x=1$
SOL: $y'=e^{-x}(1-x)=0$.
H: Product rule.||Factor $e^{-x}$.||Solve $1-x=0$.
@comet Extension: Nth derivative | If $f(x)=xe^x$, find $f^{(10)}(0)$.
A: 10
AD: $10$
SOL: $f^{(n)}(x)=(x+n)e^x$, so $f^{(10)}(0)=10$.
H: Compute $f'$, $f''$.||Pattern: $(x+n)e^x$.||Substitute.
