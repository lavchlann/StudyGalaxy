## 2.1 | Free-body diagrams and Newton's laws
@p **Newton's second law:** $\Sigma F=ma$. The net force is the vector sum of all forces. A **free-body diagram** shows only the forces acting **on** the object: weight $mg$ (down), normal force $N$ (perpendicular to the surface), friction $f$ (along the surface), applied forces and tension. **Constant velocity** means $\Sigma F=0$, not "no forces".
@key $$\Sigma F=ma,\qquad W=mg,\qquad f_k=\mu_kN,\qquad f_s\le\mu_sN$$
@ex Resultant force
Q: A $3.0\ \text{kg}$ trolley is pushed with a net force of $12\ \text{N}$. Find its acceleration.
S: $a=\frac{\Sigma F}{m}=\frac{12}{3.0}=4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
A: $4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
@warn Weight and normal force are **not** a Newton's third-law pair. A third-law pair acts on **different objects**: the Earth pulls the block down, and the block pulls the Earth up.

## 2.2 | Forces on a slope
@p On a slope of angle $\theta$, choose axes **along** and **perpendicular** to the surface. The weight splits into $mg\sin\theta$ (down the slope) and $mg\cos\theta$ (into the slope). With no acceleration perpendicular to the surface, $N=mg\cos\theta$. Down the slope, the net force is $mg\sin\theta-f$.
@key $$F_{\parallel}=mg\sin\theta,\qquad F_{\perp}=mg\cos\theta=N$$
@ex Frictionless slope
Q: A $5.0\ \text{kg}$ block sits on a frictionless $30^\circ$ slope. Find its acceleration and the normal force.
S: $a=g\sin30^\circ=4.9\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ down the slope.
S: $N=mg\cos30^\circ=5.0(9.8)(0.866)=42.4\ \text{N}$.
A: $4.9\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ down the slope and $42.4\ \text{N}$.
@int incline | Change the slope angle and the friction coefficient. The block slides only when the down-slope component beats the maximum friction.
@warn The normal force on a slope is **less than** $mg$ (it equals $mg\cos\theta$). Using $N=mg$ on a slope is one of the most common errors.

## 2.3 | Friction
@p **Static friction** adjusts up to a maximum $\mu_sN$ to stop sliding. **Kinetic friction** acts on a sliding object and equals $\mu_kN$ in the direction opposing motion. A block just on the point of sliding on a slope has $\tan\theta=\mu_s$.
@ex Sliding with friction
Q: A block slides down a $30^\circ$ slope with $\mu_k=0.30$. Find its acceleration.
S: $mg\sin\theta-\mu_kmg\cos\theta=ma$.
S: $a=9.8(\sin30^\circ-0.30\cos30^\circ)=9.8(0.500-0.260)=2.35\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
A: $2.35\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ down the slope.
@ex Critical angle
Q: A block is on a slope with $\mu_s=0.60$. Find the greatest angle before it slides.
S: $\tan\theta=\mu_s=0.60$.
A: $\theta=31.0^\circ$
@ex Pushed up a slope at constant speed
Q: A $2.0\ \text{kg}$ block is pushed up a $20^\circ$ slope at constant speed with $\mu_k=0.20$. Find the force parallel to the slope.
S: $F=mg\sin\theta+\mu_kmg\cos\theta=19.6(0.342+0.2\times0.940)=10.4\ \text{N}$.
A: $10.4\ \text{N}$ up the slope.

## 2.4 | Pulleys and connected objects
@p Treat the system as one object when the string is taut and does not stretch, so both masses share the same acceleration. For two masses over a light pulley, $a=\dfrac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}$. Then use one mass alone to find the tension.
@ex Atwood machine
Q: Masses of $5.0\ \text{kg}$ and $3.0\ \text{kg}$ hang from a light string over a pulley. Find the acceleration and the tension.
S: $a=\frac{(5.0-3.0)(9.8)}{8.0}=2.45\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
S: For the lighter mass, $T-mg=ma\Rightarrow T=3.0(9.8+2.45)=36.8\ \text{N}$.
A: $2.45\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ and $36.8\ \text{N}$.

## X | Exam toolkit
@p **Always draw the free-body diagram.** Label every force with its name and direction, show the axes along and perpendicular to the slope, and write $\Sigma F=ma$ for each axis. A correct diagram earns marks even if the arithmetic goes wrong.
@p **Units and figures.** Forces in newtons, acceleration in $\text{m s}^{-2}$, 2 or 3 significant figures. Do not round until the final line.
@quiz Normal | A $10\ \text{kg}$ object rests on level ground. Find the normal force (N).
A: 98
AD: $98\ \text{N}$
SOL: $N=mg=10(9.8)$.
H: On level ground $N=mg$.||$10\times9.8$.||Compute.
@quiz Slope component | A $4.0\ \text{kg}$ block is on a $20^\circ$ slope. Find the component of its weight down the slope (N, 1 decimal place).
A: 13.4
AD: $13.4\ \text{N}$
SOL: $mg\sin20^\circ=39.2(0.342)=13.4$.
H: Along the slope uses sine.||$mg=39.2$.||$39.2\sin20^\circ$.
@quiz Slope normal | A $4.0\ \text{kg}$ block is on a $20^\circ$ slope. Find the normal force (N, 1 decimal place).
A: 36.8
AD: $36.8\ \text{N}$
SOL: $mg\cos20^\circ=39.2(0.940)=36.8$.
H: Perpendicular uses cosine.||$mg=39.2$.||$39.2\cos20^\circ$.
@quiz Frictionless acceleration | Find the acceleration of a block on a frictionless $40^\circ$ slope ($\text{m s}^{-2}$, 2 decimal places).
A: 6.30
ALT: 6.3
AD: $6.30\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
SOL: $a=g\sin40^\circ=9.8(0.643)=6.30$.
H: Mass cancels.||$a=g\sin\theta$.||$9.8\sin40^\circ$.
@quiz Friction force | $N=40\ \text{N}$ and $\mu_k=0.25$. Find the kinetic friction (N).
A: 10
ALT: 10.0
AD: $10\ \text{N}$
SOL: $f=\mu N=0.25\times40$.
H: $f=\mu N$.||$0.25\times40$.||Compute.
@quiz Constant speed | A block slides down a $20^\circ$ slope at constant speed. Find $\mu_k$ (2 decimal places).
A: 0.36
AD: $0.36$
SOL: Net force zero: $mg\sin\theta=\mu mg\cos\theta$, so $\mu=\tan20^\circ=0.364$.
H: Constant speed means zero net force.||$\sin\theta=\mu\cos\theta$.||$\mu=\tan\theta$.
@quiz Critical angle | A block just begins to slide when the slope is $\theta$ and $\mu_s=1.0$. Find $\theta$ (degrees).
A: 45
AD: $45^\circ$
SOL: $\tan\theta=1.0$.
H: $\tan\theta=\mu_s$.||$\tan^{-1}1$.||Compute.
@quiz With friction | Find the acceleration of a block on a $30^\circ$ slope with $\mu_k=0.20$ ($\text{m s}^{-2}$, 2 decimal places).
A: 3.20
ALT: 3.2
AD: $3.20\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
SOL: $a=9.8(0.5-0.2\cos30^\circ)=9.8(0.327)=3.20$.
H: $a=g(\sin\theta-\mu\cos\theta)$.||$\cos30^\circ=0.866$.||Compute.
@quiz Weight | Find the weight of a $3.0\ \text{kg}$ object (N).
A: 29.4
ALT: 29
AD: $29.4\ \text{N}$
SOL: $W=mg=3.0(9.8)$.
H: $W=mg$.||$3.0\times9.8$.||Compute.
@quiz Newton's second law | Net force $12\ \text{N}$ acts on $3.0\ \text{kg}$. Find $a$ ($\text{m s}^{-2}$).
A: 4.0
ALT: 4
AD: $4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
SOL: $a=F/m$.
H: $a=F/m$.||$12/3$.||Compute.
@comet Extension: Atwood | Masses of $5.0\ \text{kg}$ and $3.0\ \text{kg}$ hang over a light pulley. Find the acceleration ($\text{m s}^{-2}$, 2 decimal places).
A: 2.45
AD: $2.45\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
SOL: $a=\frac{(5-3)(9.8)}{8}=2.45$.
