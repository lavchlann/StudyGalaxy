## 4.2 | Position vectors as functions of time
@p A **position vector** $\mathbf r(t)=x(t)\mathbf i+y(t)\mathbf j$ traces a **path**. To find the Cartesian equation, eliminate $t$. Circle: $\mathbf r=(a\cos t,\ a\sin t)$ gives $x^2+y^2=a^2$. Ellipse: $\mathbf r=(a\cos t,\ b\sin t)$ gives $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$. Hyperbola: $\mathbf r=(a\sec t,\ b\tan t)$ gives $\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$.
@ex Eliminating the parameter
Q: Find the Cartesian equation of $\mathbf r(t)=(2\cos t+1,\ 3\sin t-2)$.
S: $\cos t=\frac{x-1}2$, $\sin t=\frac{y+2}3$.
S: $\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1\Rightarrow\frac{(x-1)^2}4+\frac{(y+2)^2}9=1$.
A: Ellipse centred at $(1,-2)$ with semi-axes 2 and 3.
@warn Use $\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1$ on the isolated terms, not on the coefficients. Isolate $\cos t$ and $\sin t$ first.

## 4.3 | Collisions
@p Two particles with position vectors $\mathbf r_1(t)$ and $\mathbf r_2(t)$ **collide** at time $t$ when $\mathbf r_1(t)=\mathbf r_2(t)$ for the **same** $t$. Two paths can cross without a collision if the particles arrive at the intersection at different times. Equate $\mathbf i$ and $\mathbf j$ components and solve; the **same** $t$ must satisfy both.
@ex Collision?
Q: $\mathbf r_1=(2t,\ t^2)$, $\mathbf r_2=(t+2,\ 4)$. Do they collide?
S: $2t=t+2\Rightarrow t=2$. $t^2=4\Rightarrow t=\pm2$.
S: $t=2$ satisfies both.
A: Collision at $t=2$, at position $(4,4)$.

## 4.4 | Differentiating and integrating vectors
@key $$\mathbf v(t)=\mathbf r'(t)=(x'(t),\,y'(t)),\qquad\mathbf a(t)=\mathbf v'(t)=(x''(t),\,y''(t))$$
@p Differentiate and integrate **component by component**. **Speed** is $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{x'^2+y'^2}$. To integrate, add a **vector** constant, found from an initial condition: $\mathbf r(t)=\int\mathbf v\,dt+\mathbf c$.
@ex From acceleration to position
Q: $\mathbf a=(0,-10)$, $\mathbf v(0)=(6,8)$, $\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)$. Find $\mathbf r(t)$.
S: $\mathbf v=(6,\ -10t+8)$.
S: $\mathbf r=(6t,\ -5t^2+8t)$.
A: $\mathbf r(t)=(6t,\ 8t-5t^2)$

## 4.5 | Straight-line (rectilinear) motion
@p On a line, $x(t)$ is position, $v=\frac{dx}{dt}$, $a=\frac{dv}{dt}=v\frac{dv}{dx}$. The particle is **at rest** when $v=0$. **Displacement** is $x(b)-x(a)$; **distance** needs $\int|v|\,dt$. **Speeding up** when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign.
@ex Rectilinear
Q: $x=t^3-6t^2+9t$. Find when the particle is at rest, and its acceleration then.
S: $v=3t^2-12t+9=3(t-1)(t-3)=0$, so $t=1$ or $3$.
S: $a=6t-12$: $a(1)=-6$, $a(3)=6$.
A: At rest at $t=1$ (a $=-6$) and $t=3$ ($a=6$).

## 4.6 | Applications: projectiles and circular motion
@p **Projectile:** $\mathbf a=(0,-g)$, so with launch speed $u$ at angle $\alpha$, $\mathbf r=(ut\cos\alpha,\ ut\sin\alpha-\frac12gt^2)$. The **flight time** is when $y=0$ again; the **range** is $x$ at that time. **Circular motion:** $\mathbf r=(R\cos\omega t,\ R\sin\omega t)$; speed is $R\omega$ (constant), and acceleration points to the centre with magnitude $R\omega^2$.
@ex Projectile
Q: A ball is launched at $20\text{ m/s}$ at $30^\circ$ with $g=10$. Find the time of flight.
S: $y=20t\sin30^\circ-5t^2=10t-5t^2=0\Rightarrow t=2$.
A: $2$ seconds
