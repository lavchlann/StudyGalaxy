## 1.2
@ex Division
Q: Write $\dfrac{3+2i}{1-i}$ in the form $a+bi$.
S: Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $1+i$.
S: Numerator $(3+2i)(1+i)=3+3i+2i+2i^2=1+5i$; denominator $1+1=2$.
A: $\frac12+\frac52i$
## 1.3
@ex To polar form
Q: Write $z=-1+\sqrt3\,i$ in polar form.
S: $|z|=\sqrt{1+3}=2$.
S: Second quadrant: $\theta=\pi-\frac\pi3=\frac{2\pi}3$.
A: $2\,\text{cis}\frac{2\pi}3$
@ex Multiplying in polar
Q: $z_1=2\,\text{cis}\frac\pi6$, $z_2=3\,\text{cis}\frac\pi3$. Find $z_1z_2$.
S: Multiply moduli, add arguments.
A: $6\,\text{cis}\frac\pi2=6i$
@int complex | Move the two numbers and watch the green product: its length is the product of the lengths and its angle is the sum of the angles.
## 1.4
@ex De Moivre
Q: Evaluate $(1+i)^8$.
S: $1+i=\sqrt2\,\text{cis}\frac\pi4$.
S: $(\sqrt2)^8=16$, angle $8\cdot\frac\pi4=2\pi$.
A: $16$
@ex Roots
Q: Solve $z^3=8$.
S: $8=8\,\text{cis}\,0$, so $z=2\,\text{cis}\frac{2k\pi}3$ for $k=0,1,2$.
A: $z=2,\ 2\,\text{cis}\frac{2\pi}3=-1+\sqrt3 i,\ 2\,\text{cis}\frac{4\pi}3=-1-\sqrt3 i$
## 1.5
@ex Polynomial with a complex root
Q: $z^3-2z^2+4z-8=0$ has root $2i$. Find all roots.
S: Real coefficients so $-2i$ is also a root; $(z^2+4)$ is a factor.
S: Divide: $z^3-2z^2+4z-8=(z^2+4)(z-2)$.
A: $z=2,\ 2i,\ -2i$
@warn The conjugate root theorem needs **real** coefficients. A polynomial with $i$ in its coefficients need not have conjugate pairs of roots.
