## 7.2 | Bernoulli distributions
@p A **Bernoulli trial** has exactly two outcomes, "success" with probability $p$ and "failure" with probability $q=1-p$. Let $X=1$ for success and $0$ for failure. Then $E(X)=p$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=p(1-p)$.
@ex Bernoulli
Q: A bolt is defective with probability $0.04$. Find $E(X)$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)$ where $X=1$ if defective.
S: $E(X)=0.04$.
S: $\operatorname{Var}(X)=0.04\times0.96=0.0384$.
A: $0.04$ and $0.0384$

## 7.3 | The binomial distribution
@p Count the successes in $n$ **independent** Bernoulli trials with constant $p$: $X\sim\text{Bin}(n,p)$.
@key $$P(X=k)=\binom nk p^k(1-p)^{n-k},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,n$$
@p $\binom nk=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$ counts the arrangements. **Conditions for a binomial:** fixed $n$; two outcomes; constant $p$; independent trials. Use `binomPdf` for $P(X=k)$ and `binomCdf` for $P(X\le k)$ on your CAS.
@ex Binomial probability
Q: $X\sim\text{Bin}(5,0.3)$. Find $P(X=2)$ and $P(X\ge1)$.
S: $P(X=2)=\binom52(0.3)^2(0.7)^3=10(0.09)(0.343)=0.3087$.
S: $P(X\ge1)=1-P(X=0)=1-0.7^5=1-0.16807=0.83193$.
A: $0.3087$ and $0.8319$
@ex At least one
Q: A shooter hits 70% of shots. In 6 shots find $P(\text{at least 4 hits})$.
S: $P(X\ge4)=P(4)+P(5)+P(6)$ with $X\sim\text{Bin}(6,0.7)$.
S: $=0.3241+0.3025+0.1176=0.7443$.
A: $0.7443$
@warn "At least" means $\ge$; "more than" means $>$. Rewrite the inequality and choose the right complement. $P(X\ge1)=1-P(X=0)$, not $1-P(X=1)$.

## 7.4 | The mean and variance of a binomial
@key $$E(X)=np,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p),\qquad\sigma=\sqrt{np(1-p)}$$
@p These come from adding up $n$ Bernoulli variables. The distribution is symmetric when $p=0.5$, skewed right when $p<0.5$ and left when $p>0.5$.
@ex Mean and spread
Q: $X\sim\text{Bin}(50,0.2)$. Find $E(X)$ and $\sigma$.
S: $E=50(0.2)=10$.
S: $\operatorname{Var}=50(0.2)(0.8)=8$, $\sigma=\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\approx2.83$.
A: $10$ and $2\sqrt2$
@ex Finding $n$ or $p$
Q: $X\sim\text{Bin}(n,p)$ has mean $12$ and variance $9$. Find $n$ and $p$.
S: $np=12$, $np(1-p)=9\Rightarrow1-p=\frac34\Rightarrow p=\frac14$.
S: $n=\frac{12}{1/4}=48$.
A: $n=48$, $p=\frac14$

## 7.5 | Modelling with binomials
@p Check the four binomial conditions, define $X$ and its parameters in words, calculate, then **comment** on whether the model is reasonable. Independence often fails in real life (for instance, family members sharing a disease), so say so.
@ex Modelling
Q: A call centre resolves 80% of calls first time. Of 20 calls, find the probability that at least 18 are resolved first time.
S: $X\sim\text{Bin}(20,0.8)$. $P(X\ge18)=P(18)+P(19)+P(20)\approx0.1369+0.0576+0.0115=0.2060$.
A: $\approx0.206$
