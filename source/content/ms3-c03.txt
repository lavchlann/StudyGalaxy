## 3.2 | Vectors in three dimensions
@p A 3D vector is $\mathbf v=a\mathbf i+b\mathbf j+c\mathbf k=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\\c\end{pmatrix}$. Its **magnitude** is $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ and a **unit vector** is $\hat{\mathbf v}=\frac{\mathbf v}{|\mathbf v|}$. The vector from $A$ to $B$ is $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$.
@ex Vector between points
Q: $A(1,2,3)$ and $B(4,-2,5)$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $|\overrightarrow{AB}|$ and a unit vector in its direction.
S: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-4\\2\end{pmatrix}$.
S: $|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{9+16+4}=\sqrt{29}$.
A: $\overrightarrow{AB}=3\mathbf i-4\mathbf j+2\mathbf k$, $|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{29}$, $\hat{\mathbf u}=\frac1{\sqrt{29}}(3,-4,2)$.
@warn $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$ (end minus start). Reversing the order gives $\overrightarrow{BA}$, which points the other way.

## 3.3 | Vector algebra and the dot product
@key $$\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$$
@p Two vectors are **perpendicular** exactly when $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=0$ and **parallel** when one is a scalar multiple of the other. The **scalar projection** of $\mathbf a$ on $\mathbf b$ is $\frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf b|}$ and the **vector projection** is $\frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf b|^2}\mathbf b$. Also $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a=|\mathbf a|^2$.
@ex Angle between vectors
Q: Find the angle between $\mathbf a=(1,2,2)$ and $\mathbf b=(2,-1,2)$.
S: $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=2-2+4=4$; $|\mathbf a|=3$; $|\mathbf b|=3$.
S: $\cos\theta=\frac49$, so $\theta=\cos^{-1}\frac49\approx63.6^\circ$.
A: $\approx63.6^\circ$
@ex Perpendicular
Q: Find $t$ so that $(t,2,-1)\perp(3,1,t)$.
S: $3t+2-t=0\Rightarrow2t=-2\Rightarrow t=-1$.
A: $t=-1$
@warn The dot product gives a **number**, not a vector. Never write $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=\mathbf c$.

## 3.4 | Vector proofs in geometry
@p Choose position vectors for key points, express the sides as vector differences, and use the dot product (perpendicular) or scalar multiples (parallel). Classic results: the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular; the angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$; the midpoint of $AB$ has position vector $\frac12(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)$.
@ex Angle in a semicircle
Q: $AB$ is a diameter of a circle with centre $O$, and $P$ is on the circle. Show $\angle APB=90^\circ$.
S: Let $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=-\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OP}=\mathbf p$ with $|\mathbf p|=|\mathbf a|$.
S: $\overrightarrow{PA}\cdot\overrightarrow{PB}=(\mathbf a-\mathbf p)\cdot(-\mathbf a-\mathbf p)=-|\mathbf a|^2+|\mathbf p|^2=0$.
A: Perpendicular, so $\angle APB=90^\circ$.

## 3.5 | Vector, parametric and Cartesian equations
@p A **line** through point $\mathbf a$ with direction $\mathbf d$ is $\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d$. In 3D, eliminating $\lambda$ gives the Cartesian form $\frac{x-x_0}{d_1}=\frac{y-y_0}{d_2}=\frac{z-z_0}{d_3}$. Two lines in 3D can be **parallel**, **intersecting**, or **skew** (neither). To test: equate components, solve two equations, and check the third.
@ex Do the lines meet?
Q: $L_1:\ \mathbf r=(1,0,2)+\lambda(1,1,1)$ and $L_2:\ \mathbf r=(0,1,3)+\mu(2,1,0)$.
S: $1+\lambda=2\mu$, $\lambda=1+\mu$, $2+\lambda=3$.
S: Third: $\lambda=1$, then $\mu=0$; first: $2=0$? ✗.
A: They do not intersect and are not parallel, so they are skew.

## 3.6 | The cross product
@key $$\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=\begin{pmatrix}a_2b_3-a_3b_2\\a_3b_1-a_1b_3\\a_1b_2-a_2b_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta$$
@p The result is **perpendicular to both** $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ (right-hand rule), so it is a **normal** to the plane they span. $|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|$ is the **area of the parallelogram**; half of it is the area of the triangle. Order matters: $\mathbf b\times\mathbf a=-\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$.
@ex Cross product and area
Q: $\mathbf a=(1,2,3)$, $\mathbf b=(0,1,4)$. Find $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$ and the triangle area.
S: $(2\cdot4-3\cdot1,\ 3\cdot0-1\cdot4,\ 1\cdot1-2\cdot0)=(5,-4,1)$.
S: $|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|=\sqrt{25+16+1}=\sqrt{42}$, so the triangle area is $\frac{\sqrt{42}}{2}$.
A: $(5,-4,1)$; area $\frac{\sqrt{42}}2$

## 3.7 | Applications: planes and distances
@p A plane with normal $\mathbf n=(a,b,c)$ through $\mathbf p_0$ has equation $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p_0$, which expands to $ax+by+cz=d$. Get a normal from a cross product of two vectors in the plane. A line is parallel to the plane when its direction is perpendicular to $\mathbf n$.
@ex Equation of a plane
Q: Find the plane through $A(1,0,0)$, $B(0,2,0)$, $C(0,0,3)$.
S: $\overrightarrow{AB}=(-1,2,0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=(-1,0,3)$. Cross: $(6,3,2)$.
S: Plane: $6x+3y+2z=6\cdot1=6$.
A: $6x+3y+2z=6$
