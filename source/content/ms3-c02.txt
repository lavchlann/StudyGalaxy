## 2.2 | Mathematical induction
@p Induction proves a statement $P(n)$ for all integers $n\ge n_0$ in four steps: **(1) Basis:** show $P(n_0)$ is true. **(2) Assume** $P(k)$ is true for some integer $k\ge n_0$. **(3) Inductive step:** use the assumption to show $P(k+1)$ is true. **(4) Conclude:** "Since $P(n_0)$ is true and $P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)$, by induction $P(n)$ is true for all integers $n\ge n_0$."
@ex Sum of integers
Q: Prove $1+2+\dots+n=\frac{n(n+1)}2$ for all $n\ge1$.
S: Basis: $n=1$: LHS $=1$, RHS $=\frac{1\cdot2}2=1$ ✓.
S: Assume $1+\dots+k=\frac{k(k+1)}2$.
S: Then $1+\dots+k+(k+1)=\frac{k(k+1)}2+(k+1)=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}2$, which is $P(k+1)$.
S: Conclude as in step 4.
A: Proved by induction.
@warn You must use the assumption in the inductive step. If your working never uses $P(k)$, it is not an induction proof.

## 2.3 | Induction and divisibility
@p To prove "$d$ divides $f(n)$", assume $f(k)=d\,m$ for an integer $m$, and show that $f(k+1)$ is a multiple of $d$ by rewriting $f(k+1)$ in terms of $f(k)$.
@ex Divisibility
Q: Prove $7^n-1$ is divisible by 6 for $n\ge1$.
S: Basis: $7-1=6$ ✓.
S: Assume $7^k-1=6m$, so $7^k=6m+1$.
S: $7^{k+1}-1=7(6m+1)-1=42m+6=6(7m+1)$ ✓.
A: Proved.

## 2.4 | Trigonometric proofs with De Moivre
@p De Moivre says $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n=\cos n\theta+i\sin n\theta$. Expand the left with the binomial theorem and equate **real** and **imaginary** parts to get multiple-angle identities.
@ex Triple angle
Q: Show $\cos3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta$.
S: $(c+is)^3=c^3+3ic^2s-3cs^2-is^3$, with $c=\cos\theta$, $s=\sin\theta$.
S: Real part: $c^3-3cs^2=c^3-3c(1-c^2)=4c^3-3c$.
A: $\cos3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta$
