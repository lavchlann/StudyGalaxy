## 4.2
@p **What this topic is about.** Describing the motion of an object by a position vector that changes with time, $\mathbf r(t)$. You will sketch paths, convert to Cartesian equations, and find where an object is at a given time.
@key $\mathbf r(t)=x(t)\mathbf i+y(t)\mathbf j\ (+z(t)\mathbf k)$ gives the position at time $t$. The **path** (Cartesian equation) is found by **eliminating $t$** between $x(t)$ and $y(t)$. Always state any domain or range restrictions that come from $t$.
@p **Eliminating $t$.** (1) If one coordinate is simple, make $t$ the subject and substitute: $x=t+1\Rightarrow t=x-1$. (2) If both involve $\sin$ and $\cos$ of the same angle, use $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$: $x=3\cos t$, $y=3\sin t\Rightarrow\left(\frac x3\right)^2+\left(\frac y3\right)^2=1\Rightarrow x^2+y^2=9$. (3) If $x=a\cos t$, $y=b\sin t$, you get an ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$.
@p **Restrictions.** If $x=t^2$, then $x\ge0$, so only part of the curve is traced. If $t\in[0,\pi]$ with $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, only the upper semicircle is traced. Markers expect you to state these.
@p **Direction of motion.** Make a small table of $t$, $x$, $y$ values and plot points in order, adding arrows. For $\cos t,\sin t$ with increasing $t$ the motion is anticlockwise.
@ex Circle (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r(t)=3\cos t\,\mathbf i+3\sin t\,\mathbf j$, $t\ge0$. Find the Cartesian equation and describe the motion.
S: **Isolate trig:** $\cos t=\frac x3$, $\sin t=\frac y3$.
S: **Identity:** $\frac{x^2}9+\frac{y^2}9=1\Rightarrow x^2+y^2=9$.
S: **Start:** $t=0$ gives $(3,0)$; $t=\frac\pi2$ gives $(0,3)$.
S: **Describe:** circle of radius 3 centred at the origin, travelled anticlockwise starting at $(3,0)$, period $2\pi$.
A: $x^2+y^2=9$, anticlockwise
@ex Parabola with restriction (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r(t)=t^2\mathbf i+(t^2+2)\mathbf j$, $t\in\mathbb R$. Find the path.
S: **Eliminate:** $x=t^2$, so $y=x+2$.
S: **Restriction:** $x=t^2\ge0$.
S: **Path:** the half-line $y=x+2$ for $x\ge0$ (starting at $(0,2)$).
A: $y=x+2$, $x\ge0$
@ex Ellipse (full working)
Q: Find the Cartesian equation of $\mathbf r(t)=4\cos t\,\mathbf i+2\sin t\,\mathbf j$.
S: **Isolate:** $\cos t=\frac x4$, $\sin t=\frac y2$.
S: **Identity:** $\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}4=1$.
S: **Shape:** ellipse with $x$-intercepts $\pm4$ and $y$-intercepts $\pm2$.
A: $\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}4=1$
@ex Position at a time (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r(t)=(t^2-1)\mathbf i+(3t)\mathbf j$. Find the position at $t=2$ and its distance from the origin.
S: **Substitute:** $x=4-1=3$, $y=6$.
S: **Position:** $(3,6)$.
S: **Distance:** $\sqrt{9+36}=\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt5$.
A: $(3,6)$, $3\sqrt5$

## 4.3
@p **What this topic is about.** Deciding whether two moving objects collide (are at the same place **at the same time**) or whether their paths merely cross (same place, different times). Also finding the closest approach.
@key **Collision:** solve $\mathbf r_1(t)=\mathbf r_2(t)$ with the **same** $t$ in both. Equate each component; a single $t$ must satisfy **every** equation. **Paths cross:** solve $\mathbf r_1(t)=\mathbf r_2(s)$ with **different** parameters.
@p **Method.** (1) Write each component equation. (2) Solve one for $t$. (3) Check that value in the other equation(s). If it works: collision at that time; find the point. If not: no collision (but the paths may still cross; test with $t$ and $s$).
@p **Closest approach.** The separation is $\mathbf d(t)=\mathbf r_2(t)-\mathbf r_1(t)$. Minimise $|\mathbf d|^2$ (no square root to differentiate): set $\frac{d}{dt}|\mathbf d|^2=0$, or complete the square.
@ex Collision (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r_1=(t,2t)$ and $\mathbf r_2=(3-t,3)$. Do the objects collide?
S: **$x$:** $t=3-t\Rightarrow t=1.5$.
S: **$y$:** $2t=3\Rightarrow t=1.5$.
S: **Same time:** both give $t=1.5$ ✓.
S: **Point:** $\mathbf r_1(1.5)=(1.5,3)$.
A: Collide at $t=1.5$ at $(1.5,3)$
@ex Paths cross but no collision (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r_1=(t,t)$ and $\mathbf r_2=(4-t,2)$. Collision? Do the paths cross?
S: **Same time, $x$:** $t=4-t\Rightarrow t=2$. **$y$:** $t=2$ ✓. So they collide at $(2,2)$.
S: **Compare** with $\mathbf r_2=(4-t,1)$: $x$ gives $t=2$ but $y$ gives $t=1$, so no collision.
S: **Paths:** $\mathbf r_1(t)=\mathbf r_2(s)$: $t=4-s$ and $t=1$, so $s=3$. Paths cross at $(1,1)$ at different times.
A: First pair collides at $(2,2)$; second pair's paths cross at $(1,1)$ but no collision
@ex Closest approach (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r_1=(t,0)$ and $\mathbf r_2=(0,t-2)$. Find the minimum distance.
S: **Separation:** $\mathbf d=(-t,t-2)$.
S: **Squared distance:** $D=t^2+(t-2)^2=2t^2-4t+4$.
S: **Minimise:** $D'=4t-4=0\Rightarrow t=1$; $D(1)=2-4+4=2$.
S: **Distance:** $\sqrt2$.
A: Minimum distance $\sqrt2$ at $t=1$
@ex Collision in 3D (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r_1=(1+t,2t,3)$, $\mathbf r_2=(5-t,4,1+t)$. Collision?
S: **$x$:** $1+t=5-t\Rightarrow t=2$.
S: **$y$:** $2t=4\Rightarrow t=2$ ✓.
S: **$z$:** $3=1+t\Rightarrow t=2$ ✓.
S: **Point:** $(3,4,3)$.
A: Collide at $t=2$ at $(3,4,3)$
@warn Same point is not enough. A collision needs the same point at the **same time**.

## 4.4
@p **What this topic is about.** Differentiating and integrating position vectors to get velocity and acceleration, and working backwards from acceleration to position with initial conditions. Speed is the magnitude of velocity.
@key $$\mathbf v=\dot{\mathbf r}=\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt}\qquad\mathbf a=\ddot{\mathbf r}=\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}\qquad\text{speed}=|\mathbf v|$$ Differentiate or integrate **each component separately**. When integrating, include a **vector** constant $\mathbf c$ and find it from an initial condition.
@p **Interpreting.** $\mathbf v$ is tangent to the path and points in the direction of motion. The object is momentarily at rest when $\mathbf v=\mathbf 0$ (all components zero at the same time). Maximum or minimum speed: maximise $|\mathbf v|^2$.
@p **Integration with initial conditions.** $\mathbf v(t)=\int\mathbf a\,dt=(\text{antiderivatives})+\mathbf c$. Substitute $t=0$ (or the given time) and the given vector to find $\mathbf c$. Repeat to go from $\mathbf v$ to $\mathbf r$.
@ex Velocity and speed (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r=(t^2,3t)$. Find $\mathbf v(2)$ and the speed at $t=2$.
S: **Differentiate:** $\mathbf v=(2t,3)$.
S: **Substitute:** $\mathbf v(2)=(4,3)$.
S: **Speed:** $\sqrt{16+9}=5$.
A: $\mathbf v(2)=(4,3)$; speed 5
@ex Acceleration (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r=(t^3,2t^2)$. Find $\mathbf a(1)$.
S: **Velocity:** $\mathbf v=(3t^2,4t)$.
S: **Acceleration:** $\mathbf a=(6t,4)$.
S: **At $t=1$:** $(6,4)$.
A: $(6,4)$
@ex Integrating to position (full working)
Q: $\mathbf v=(2t,6t^2)$ and $\mathbf r(0)=(1,0)$. Find $\mathbf r(1)$.
S: **Integrate:** $\mathbf r=(t^2,2t^3)+\mathbf c$.
S: **Initial condition:** $\mathbf r(0)=\mathbf c=(1,0)$.
S: **Position:** $\mathbf r=(t^2+1,2t^3)$.
S: **At $t=1$:** $(2,2)$.
A: $(2,2)$
@ex Speed on a circle (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r=(5\cos2t,5\sin2t)$. Find the speed and show $\mathbf v\perp\mathbf r$.
S: **Velocity (chain rule):** $\mathbf v=(-10\sin2t,10\cos2t)$.
S: **Speed:** $\sqrt{100\sin^22t+100\cos^22t}=10$ (constant).
S: **Dot:** $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf v=-50\cos2t\sin2t+50\sin2t\cos2t=0$.
S: **So** velocity is perpendicular to the radius, as expected for circular motion.
A: Speed 10; $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf v=0$
@warn Integrating needs a constant vector. Forgetting it, or finding only one component of it, loses marks.

## 4.5
@p **What this topic is about.** Motion along a straight line: displacement $x$, velocity $v$ and acceleration $a$ as functions of time. You will also use the extra forms of acceleration that let you work with $v$ as a function of $x$.
@key $$v=\frac{dx}{dt},\quad a=\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}=v\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac12v^2\right)$$ **At rest:** $v=0$. **Displacement** over $[t_1,t_2]$: $\int v\,dt$. **Distance travelled:** $\int|v|\,dt$ (split at times when $v=0$).
@p **Choosing the form.** If $a$ is given in terms of $t$, integrate $\frac{dv}{dt}=a(t)$. If $a$ is given in terms of $x$, use $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac12v^2\right)=a(x)$ and integrate with respect to $x$. If $a$ is in terms of $v$, use $\frac{dv}{dt}=a(v)$ (separate) or $v\frac{dv}{dx}=a(v)$.
@p **Speeding up or slowing down.** Speeding up when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign; slowing down when opposite signs.
@ex At rest and distance (full working)
Q: $x=t^2-4t$ for $0\le t\le5$. When is the particle at rest, and how far does it travel?
S: **Velocity:** $v=2t-4=0$ at $t=2$.
S: **Positions:** $x(0)=0$, $x(2)=-4$, $x(5)=5$.
S: **Distance:** $|{-4}-0|+|5-(-4)|=4+9=13$.
S: **Displacement** (for comparison): $5-0=5$.
A: At rest at $t=2$; distance 13
@ex Displacement from velocity (full working)
Q: $v=2t$ for $0\le t\le3$. Find the displacement.
S: **Integrate:** $\int_0^3 2t\,dt=[t^2]_0^3$.
S: **Evaluate:** $9-0=9$.
S: **Note:** $v\ge0$ throughout, so distance also 9.
A: 9
@ex Acceleration as a function of $x$ (full working)
Q: $a=-4x$ and $v=6$ when $x=0$. Find $v^2$ in terms of $x$.
S: **Form:** $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac12v^2\right)=-4x$.
S: **Integrate:** $\frac12v^2=-2x^2+c$.
S: **Condition:** $\frac12(36)=c\Rightarrow c=18$.
S: **Result:** $v^2=36-4x^2$. (Motion is between $x=\pm3$, where $v=0$.)
A: $v^2=36-4x^2$
@ex Acceleration in terms of $v$ (full working)
Q: $\frac{dv}{dt}=-2v$ with $v(0)=10$. Find $v(t)$.
S: **Separate:** $\frac{dv}{v}=-2\,dt$.
S: **Integrate:** $\ln v=-2t+c$.
S: **Condition:** $\ln10=c$.
S: **Result:** $v=10e^{-2t}$.
A: $v=10e^{-2t}$
@warn Distance is not displacement. If $v$ changes sign, split the interval at the turning time.

## 4.6
@p **What this topic is about.** Two key applications: **projectile motion** (constant downward acceleration $g$) and **uniform circular motion**. Both are modelled with vector calculus from 4.4.
@key **Projectile** launched at speed $V$ and angle $\theta$, with $\mathbf a=(0,-g)$: $$\mathbf v=(V\cos\theta,\ V\sin\theta-gt),\qquad\mathbf r=(Vt\cos\theta,\ Vt\sin\theta-\tfrac12gt^2)$$ (from the origin). **Max height** when $\dot y=0$. **Time of flight** when $y=0$ ($t>0$). **Range** $=\frac{V^2\sin2\theta}g$, maximum at $\theta=45^\circ$.
@key **Circular motion** $\mathbf r=(R\cos\omega t,R\sin\omega t)$: speed $R\omega$, acceleration $\mathbf a=-\omega^2\mathbf r$ (towards the centre), magnitude $R\omega^2$, period $\frac{2\pi}\omega$.
@p **Projectile method.** (1) Set up $\mathbf a$ and integrate twice using the initial velocity and position. (2) Use the vertical component for heights and flight times. (3) Use the horizontal component for distances. (4) Answer with units.
@ex Projectile (full working)
Q: A ball is thrown from ground level with initial velocity $(20,15)$ m/s and $g=10$. Find the time of flight, maximum height and range.
S: **Position:** $\mathbf r=(20t,\ 15t-5t^2)$.
S: **Flight:** $15t-5t^2=0\Rightarrow5t(3-t)=0\Rightarrow t=3$ s.
S: **Max height:** $\dot y=15-10t=0$ at $t=1.5$; $y=22.5-11.25=11.25$ m.
S: **Range:** $x(3)=60$ m.
A: 3 s, 11.25 m, 60 m
@ex Height reached (full working)
Q: Height $y=10t-5t^2$. Find the maximum height.
S: **Differentiate:** $\dot y=10-10t$.
S: **Zero:** $t=1$.
S: **Height:** $10-5=5$.
A: 5 m
@ex Circular motion (full working)
Q: $\mathbf r=(5\cos2t,5\sin2t)$. Find the speed, acceleration magnitude and period.
S: **Identify:** $R=5$, $\omega=2$.
S: **Speed:** $R\omega=10$.
S: **Acceleration:** $\mathbf a=-4\mathbf r$, magnitude $R\omega^2=20$.
S: **Period:** $\frac{2\pi}2=\pi$.
A: Speed 10, $|\mathbf a|=20$, period $\pi$
@ex Maximum range (full working)
Q: Launch speed 20 m/s, $g=10$. Find the maximum range.
S: **Range:** $R=\frac{V^2\sin2\theta}{g}=\frac{400\sin2\theta}{10}=40\sin2\theta$.
S: **Maximum:** $\sin2\theta=1$ at $\theta=45^\circ$.
S: **Value:** 40 m.
A: 40 m at $45^\circ$
