## 5.2 | Matrix algebra, determinants and inverses
@p **Addition** is entry by entry (same size). **Scalar multiple** scales every entry. **Product** $AB$ requires columns of $A$ = rows of $B$; entry $(i,j)$ is row $i$ of $A$ dotted with column $j$ of $B$. Multiplication is **not commutative**: $AB\ne BA$ in general.
@key For $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$: $\det A=ad-bc$ and $A^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}$ when $\det A\ne0$. For any invertible matrices, $(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}$.
@ex Inverse
Q: Find the inverse of $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\5&3\end{pmatrix}$.
S: $\det A=6-5=1$.
S: $A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-5&2\end{pmatrix}$.
A: $A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-5&2\end{pmatrix}$
@ex Solving $AX=B$
Q: Solve $A\mathbf x=\begin{pmatrix}4\\9\end{pmatrix}$ for $A$ above.
S: $\mathbf x=A^{-1}\mathbf b=\begin{pmatrix}3(4)-9\\-5(4)+2(9)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-2\end{pmatrix}$.
A: $x=3,\ y=-2$
@warn Multiply by the inverse on the **same side** on both sides. If $AX=B$ then $X=A^{-1}B$, not $BA^{-1}$.

## 5.3 | Gaussian elimination
@p Write the system as an **augmented matrix** and use row operations (swap rows, scale a row, add a multiple of one row to another) to reach **row echelon form**, then **back-substitute**. Keep track of each operation, for example $R_2\to R_2-2R_1$.
@ex Solving a 3×3 system
Q: Solve $x+y+z=6$, $2y+5z=-4$, $2x+5y-z=27$.
S: Eliminate $x$ from row 3: $R_3\to R_3-2R_1$: $3y-3z=15\Rightarrow y-z=5$.
S: Now $2y+5z=-4$ and $y-z=5$. $2(5+z)+5z=-4\Rightarrow7z=-14\Rightarrow z=-2$.
S: $y=3$, $x=6-3+2=5$.
A: $(x,y,z)=(5,3,-2)$

## 5.4 | The three cases
@p A system of linear equations has **one** solution, **no** solution, or **infinitely many**. After elimination, a row $0=k$ with $k\ne0$ means **no solution** (inconsistent). A row $0=0$ leaves a free variable: let it be a parameter $t$ to describe the **infinite** solution set. For a square system, $\det A=0$ means it is **not** the unique case.
@ex Parameter
Q: $x+y+z=3$ and $x-y+z=1$. Describe all solutions.
S: Adding: $2x+2z=4$, so $x=2-z$. Subtracting: $2y=2$, so $y=1$.
S: Let $z=t$: $x=2-t$.
A: $(2-t,\ 1,\ t)$, a line of solutions.
@ex Finding the parameter
Q: For what $k$ does $x+2y=3$, $2x+4y=k$ have no solution?
S: Second $-2\times$ first: $0=k-6$.
A: No solution when $k\ne6$; infinitely many when $k=6$.

## 5.5 | Dominance and Leslie matrices
@p A **dominance matrix** has entry $1$ if $i$ beats $j$ (else $0$). One-step dominance counts direct wins; $A+A^2$ counts one- and two-step wins. A **Leslie matrix** models age-structured populations: the top row holds **fertility** rates and the sub-diagonal holds **survival** rates. If $\mathbf x_n$ is the population vector, $\mathbf x_{n+1}=L\mathbf x_n$ and $\mathbf x_n=L^n\mathbf x_0$.
@ex Leslie matrix
Q: $L=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0.5&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf x_0=\begin{pmatrix}100\\40\end{pmatrix}$. Find $\mathbf x_1$.
S: $\mathbf x_1=\begin{pmatrix}0(100)+2(40)\\0.5(100)+0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}80\\50\end{pmatrix}$.
A: $\begin{pmatrix}80\\50\end{pmatrix}$

## 5.6 | Applications
@p A **transition matrix** $T$ (columns sum to 1) takes a state vector forward: $\mathbf s_{n+1}=T\mathbf s_n$. The **steady state** satisfies $T\mathbf s=\mathbf s$ with entries summing to 1. Matrices also encode and decode messages: encode with $E$, decode with $E^{-1}$.
@ex Steady state
Q: $T=\begin{pmatrix}0.8&0.3\\0.2&0.7\end{pmatrix}$. Find the steady state.
S: $0.8s_1+0.3s_2=s_1\Rightarrow0.3s_2=0.2s_1\Rightarrow s_1=1.5s_2$, with $s_1+s_2=1$.
S: $s_2=0.4$, $s_1=0.6$.
A: $\begin{pmatrix}0.6\\0.4\end{pmatrix}$
