## 3.1 | Uniform circular motion
@p In **uniform circular motion** the speed is constant but the **direction changes**, so the velocity changes and the object **accelerates toward the centre**. For a radius $r$ and period $T$ (time for one revolution): $v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}=2\pi rf$ where $f=\frac1T$ is the frequency in hertz. Angular speed $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ in $\text{rad s}^{-1}$ and $v=\omega r$.
@key $$v=\frac{2\pi r}{T},\qquad a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}=\omega^2r$$
@ex Speed and acceleration
Q: A stone on a $0.80\ \text{m}$ string is whirled at $2.0$ revolutions per second. Find its speed and centripetal acceleration.
S: $T=0.50\ \text{s}$; $v=\frac{2\pi(0.80)}{0.50}=10.1\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
S: $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{10.05^2}{0.80}=126\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
A: $10.1\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ and $126\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ toward the centre.
@warn "Centrifugal force" is not a real force on the object. The object would move in a straight line at a tangent if the inward force vanished. Always name the real force providing the centripetal force (tension, friction, normal force, gravity).

## 3.2 | Centripetal force
@p **Centripetal force** is the **net** force toward the centre: $F_c=ma_c=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$. It is not a new force; it is the resultant of the real forces. On a flat curve it is **friction**; on a string it is **tension**; for a planet it is **gravity**.
@ex Car on a flat curve
Q: A $1200\ \text{kg}$ car goes around a flat curve of radius $50\ \text{m}$ at $15\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Find the friction force needed and the minimum coefficient of friction.
S: $F=\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{1200(225)}{50}=5400\ \text{N}$.
S: $\mu mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}\Rightarrow\mu=\frac{v^2}{rg}=\frac{225}{50(9.8)}=0.46$.
A: $5400\ \text{N}$ and $\mu\ge0.46$.

## 3.3 | Banked curves and horizontal circles
@p On a **frictionless banked curve**, the horizontal component of the normal force provides the centripetal force: $N\sin\theta=\frac{mv^2}{r}$ and $N\cos\theta=mg$. Dividing gives $\tan\theta=\dfrac{v^2}{rg}$. The same method works for a conical pendulum (tension has horizontal and vertical components).
@ex Banked road
Q: At what angle should a frictionless curve of radius $80\ \text{m}$ be banked for $20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$?
S: $\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}=\frac{400}{80(9.8)}=0.510$.
A: $\theta=27.0^\circ$
@p **Method:** resolve forces vertically (no acceleration) and horizontally toward the centre ($F=mv^2/r$), then combine.

## 3.4 | Vertical circles
@p In a vertical circle, speed changes and the net inward force changes with position. **Top:** $T+mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}$. **Bottom:** $T-mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}$. The minimum speed at the top for a string to stay taut is when $T=0$: $v_{\min}=\sqrt{gr}$. On a hill or bridge, $mg-N=\frac{mv^2}{r}$ at the top, so you feel lighter.
@ex Bottom of a swing
Q: A $2.0\ \text{kg}$ mass moves at $3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at the bottom of a vertical circle of radius $1.5\ \text{m}$. Find the tension.
S: $T-mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}$, so $T=2.0\left(9.8+\frac{9.0}{1.5}\right)=31.6\ \text{N}$.
A: $31.6\ \text{N}$
@ex Minimum speed
Q: Find the minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle of radius $0.50\ \text{m}$.
S: $T=0\Rightarrow mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{gr}=\sqrt{4.9}=2.21\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
A: $2.21\ \text{m s}^{-1}$

## X | Exam toolkit
@p **Say which force.** Examiners want the real force supplying the centripetal force, and its direction toward the centre. "Centripetal force" alone as a labelled arrow on a free-body diagram is usually wrong.
@p **Check your signs.** Take the direction toward the centre as positive for circular-motion equations. At the top of a circle gravity points toward the centre; at the bottom it points away.
@quiz Speed | A stone moves in a circle of radius $2.0\ \text{m}$ with period $4.0\ \text{s}$. Find its speed ($\text{m s}^{-1}$, 2 decimal places).
A: 3.14
AD: $3.14\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
SOL: $v=\frac{2\pi(2.0)}{4.0}=3.14$.
H: $v=2\pi r/T$.||$2\pi\times2.0=12.57$.||Divide by 4.0.

@quiz Bottom tension | $m=2.0\ \text{kg}$, $v=3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, $r=1.5\ \text{m}$ at the bottom of a vertical circle. Find the tension (N, 1 decimal place).
A: 31.6
AD: $31.6\ \text{N}$
SOL: $T=m(g+v^2/r)=2.0(9.8+6.0)$.
H: Tension minus weight gives centripetal force.||$T=mg+mv^2/r$.||Substitute.
@quiz Hill | A $1000\ \text{kg}$ car crosses the top of a hill of radius $40\ \text{m}$ at $10\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Find the normal force (N).
A: 7300
AD: $7300\ \text{N}$
SOL: $N=m(g-v^2/r)=1000(9.8-2.5)=7300$.
H: Weight minus normal gives the inward force.||$mg-N=mv^2/r$.||Substitute.
