## 3.2
@p **What this topic is about.** Extending 2D vectors to three dimensions. You need to read and write 3D vectors, find their lengths, the vector between two points, unit vectors and midpoints. Everything later in the chapter (lines, planes, cross products) is built from these basics.
@key A 3D vector is $\mathbf a=a_1\mathbf i+a_2\mathbf j+a_3\mathbf k=(a_1,a_2,a_3)$, where $\mathbf i,\mathbf j,\mathbf k$ are unit vectors along the $x$, $y$, $z$ axes. **Magnitude:** $|\mathbf a|=\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2}$. **Unit vector:** $\hat{\mathbf a}=\frac{\mathbf a}{|\mathbf a|}$.
@p **Position vectors and displacement.** The position vector of point $A$ is $\overrightarrow{OA}$, the vector from the origin to $A$. The vector from $A$ to $B$ is $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA}$ ("end minus start"). Its magnitude is the distance $AB$.
@p **3D axes.** The axes follow the right-hand rule: point your fingers along $x$, curl them to $y$, and your thumb points along $z$. The coordinate planes are $z=0$ (the $xy$-plane), $y=0$ and $x=0$.
@p **Midpoint and dividing a segment.** The midpoint $M$ of $AB$ has $\overrightarrow{OM}=\frac12(\overrightarrow{OA}+\overrightarrow{OB})$. The point $P$ dividing $AB$ in ratio $m:n$ has $\overrightarrow{OP}=\overrightarrow{OA}+\frac m{m+n}\overrightarrow{AB}$.
@ex Magnitude and unit vector (full working)
Q: Find $|\mathbf a|$ and $\hat{\mathbf a}$ for $\mathbf a=2\mathbf i-3\mathbf j+6\mathbf k$.
S: **Square components:** $4+9+36=49$.
S: **Magnitude:** $|\mathbf a|=\sqrt{49}=7$.
S: **Unit vector:** $\hat{\mathbf a}=\frac17(2\mathbf i-3\mathbf j+6\mathbf k)$.
S: **Check:** $\frac{4+9+36}{49}=1$ ✓.
A: $7$; $\frac17(2,-3,6)$
@ex Distance between points (full working)
Q: $A(1,1,1)$ and $B(3,4,1)$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $AB$.
S: **End minus start:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(3-1,4-1,1-1)=(2,3,0)$.
S: **Magnitude:** $\sqrt{4+9+0}=\sqrt{13}$.
A: $(2,3,0)$; $\sqrt{13}$
@ex Dividing a segment (full working)
Q: $A(2,0,-1)$, $B(8,3,5)$. Find the point $P$ dividing $AB$ in the ratio $1:2$.
S: **Vector $AB$:** $(6,3,6)$.
S: **Fraction of the way:** $\frac1{1+2}=\frac13$.
S: **Position:** $\overrightarrow{OP}=(2,0,-1)+\frac13(6,3,6)=(2,0,-1)+(2,1,2)$.
S: **Result:** $(4,1,1)$.
A: $P(4,1,1)$
@ex Vector of a given length (full working)
Q: Find a vector of length 10 in the direction of $(1,2,2)$.
S: **Magnitude:** $\sqrt{1+4+4}=3$.
S: **Unit vector:** $\frac13(1,2,2)$.
S: **Scale by 10:** $\frac{10}3(1,2,2)=\left(\frac{10}3,\frac{20}3,\frac{20}3\right)$.
A: $\frac{10}3(1,2,2)$
@warn $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$, not $\mathbf a-\mathbf b$. Getting it backwards reverses the direction.

## 3.3
@p **What this topic is about.** The **dot (scalar) product** multiplies two vectors to give a number. It finds angles between vectors, tests perpendicularity, and gives projections (how much of one vector points along another).
@key $$\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$$ **Perpendicular:** $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=0$. **Angle:** $\cos\theta=\frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|}$. **Scalar projection** of $\mathbf a$ on $\mathbf b$: $\mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf b}$. **Vector projection:** $(\mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf b})\hat{\mathbf b}$.
@p **Properties.** $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf a$; $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a=|\mathbf a|^2$; $\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b+\mathbf c)=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c$. A positive dot product means an acute angle; negative means obtuse.
@p **Parallel vectors.** $\mathbf a\parallel\mathbf b$ when $\mathbf a=\lambda\mathbf b$ for some scalar $\lambda$. Check by dividing matching components: they must all give the same ratio.
@p **Direction cosines.** The angles $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ that $\mathbf a$ makes with the axes satisfy $\cos\alpha=\frac{a_1}{|\mathbf a|}$, etc., and $\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\beta+\cos^2\gamma=1$.
@ex Angle between vectors (full working)
Q: Find the angle between $\mathbf a=(1,2,2)$ and $\mathbf b=(2,-1,2)$.
S: **Dot product:** $2-2+4=4$.
S: **Magnitudes:** $|\mathbf a|=3$, $|\mathbf b|=3$.
S: **Cosine:** $\cos\theta=\frac4{9}$.
S: **Angle:** $\theta=\cos^{-1}\frac49\approx63.6^\circ$.
A: $\approx63.6^\circ$
@ex Perpendicular, find $k$ (full working)
Q: Find $k$ so that $(k,3,1)\perp(2,1,-5)$.
S: **Condition:** dot product $=0$.
S: **Compute:** $2k+3-5=0$.
S: **Solve:** $2k=2$, $k=1$.
A: $k=1$
@ex Projections (full working)
Q: Find the scalar and vector projections of $\mathbf a=(3,1,4)$ onto $\mathbf b=(1,2,2)$.
S: **Unit vector:** $|\mathbf b|=3$, $\hat{\mathbf b}=\frac13(1,2,2)$.
S: **Scalar projection:** $\mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf b}=\frac13(3+2+8)=\frac{13}3$.
S: **Vector projection:** $\frac{13}3\times\frac13(1,2,2)=\frac{13}9(1,2,2)$.
A: $\frac{13}3$; $\frac{13}9(1,2,2)$
@ex Perpendicular component (full working)
Q: Write $\mathbf a=(3,1,4)$ as a vector parallel to $\mathbf b=(1,2,2)$ plus a vector perpendicular to it.
S: **Parallel part:** $\frac{13}9(1,2,2)$ (from the previous example).
S: **Perpendicular part:** $(3,1,4)-\frac{13}9(1,2,2)=\frac19(27-13,9-26,36-26)=\frac19(14,-17,10)$.
S: **Check:** $\frac19(14-34+20)=0$ ✓ perpendicular to $\mathbf b$.
A: $\frac{13}9(1,2,2)+\frac19(14,-17,10)$
@warn The scalar projection uses the **unit** vector $\hat{\mathbf b}$. Forgetting to divide by $|\mathbf b|$ is the common mistake.

## 3.4
@p **What this topic is about.** Using vectors to prove geometric facts: that lines are parallel, points are collinear, diagonals bisect each other, or angles are right angles. These are communication-heavy questions where every step must be justified.
@p **Toolkit.** **Parallel:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=\lambda\overrightarrow{CD}$. **Collinear points** $A,B,C$: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\lambda\overrightarrow{AC}$ (parallel with a shared point). **Perpendicular:** dot product 0. **Equal lengths:** $|\mathbf u|^2=|\mathbf v|^2$, often via $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u$. **Midpoint:** $\frac12(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)$.
@p **Setting up.** Choose an origin (often a vertex). Name the basic vectors, e.g. $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf b$. Express every other vector in terms of these by "walking" around the figure: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{AO}+\overrightarrow{OB}=-\mathbf a+\mathbf b$.
@p **Using $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u=|\mathbf u|^2$.** To show something involving lengths and angles, expand dot products like brackets: $(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)\cdot(\mathbf a-\mathbf b)=|\mathbf a|^2-|\mathbf b|^2$. This one line proves that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.
@ex Midpoint theorem (full working)
Q: In triangle $OAB$, $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of $OA$ and $OB$. Prove $MN\parallel AB$ and $MN=\frac12AB$.
S: **Basis:** $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf b$.
S: **Midpoints:** $\overrightarrow{OM}=\frac12\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{ON}=\frac12\mathbf b$.
S: **Vectors:** $\overrightarrow{MN}=\frac12\mathbf b-\frac12\mathbf a=\frac12(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$.
S: **Conclude:** $\overrightarrow{MN}=\frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$, so $MN\parallel AB$ and $MN=\frac12AB$.
A: Proved
@ex Rhombus diagonals (full working)
Q: $OACB$ is a rhombus with $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf b$, $|\mathbf a|=|\mathbf b|$. Prove its diagonals are perpendicular.
S: **Diagonals:** $\overrightarrow{OC}=\mathbf a+\mathbf b$ and $\overrightarrow{BA}=\mathbf a-\mathbf b$.
S: **Dot product:** $(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)\cdot(\mathbf a-\mathbf b)=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b+\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf a-\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf b$.
S: **Simplify:** $=|\mathbf a|^2-|\mathbf b|^2=0$ since the sides are equal.
S: **Conclude:** dot product 0, so the diagonals are perpendicular.
A: Proved
@ex Collinear points (full working)
Q: Show that $A(1,2,3)$, $B(3,5,4)$ and $C(7,11,6)$ are collinear.
S: **Vectors:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(2,3,1)$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=(6,9,3)$.
S: **Compare:** $(6,9,3)=3(2,3,1)$, so $\overrightarrow{AC}=3\overrightarrow{AB}$.
S: **Conclude:** parallel with common point $A$, so collinear.
A: Collinear
@ex Angle in a semicircle (full working)
Q: $AB$ is a diameter of a circle with centre $O$ and $P$ is on the circle. Prove $\angle APB=90^\circ$.
S: **Basis:** $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=-\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OP}=\mathbf p$, with $|\mathbf p|=|\mathbf a|$ (radii).
S: **Vectors:** $\overrightarrow{PA}=\mathbf a-\mathbf p$, $\overrightarrow{PB}=-\mathbf a-\mathbf p$.
S: **Dot:** $(\mathbf a-\mathbf p)\cdot(-\mathbf a-\mathbf p)=-|\mathbf a|^2-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf p+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf a+|\mathbf p|^2=|\mathbf p|^2-|\mathbf a|^2=0$.
S: **Conclude:** $\angle APB=90^\circ$.
A: Proved
@warn Parallel vectors alone do not prove collinearity. You must also mention a shared point.

## 3.5
@p **What this topic is about.** Describing lines in 3D. A line is fixed by a point on it and a direction. You will write the vector, parametric and Cartesian equations, and decide whether two lines are parallel, intersecting or skew.
@key **Vector equation:** $\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d$ (point $\mathbf a$, direction $\mathbf d$). **Parametric:** $x=a_1+\lambda d_1$, $y=a_2+\lambda d_2$, $z=a_3+\lambda d_3$. **Cartesian:** $\frac{x-a_1}{d_1}=\frac{y-a_2}{d_2}=\frac{z-a_3}{d_3}$.
@p **Line through two points.** Use one point as $\mathbf a$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}$ as $\mathbf d$.
@p **Two lines in 3D.** (1) If directions are parallel: the lines are **parallel** (or the same line if they share a point). (2) Otherwise set the parametric forms equal: three equations, two unknowns $\lambda,\mu$. Solve two of them and **test the third**. If it holds: **intersecting** (substitute to find the point). If not: **skew** (not parallel, never meet).
@p **Angle between lines** uses the direction vectors: $\cos\theta=\frac{|\mathbf d_1\cdot\mathbf d_2|}{|\mathbf d_1||\mathbf d_2|}$ (absolute value gives the acute angle).
@ex Line through two points (full working)
Q: Find vector and Cartesian equations of the line through $A(1,1,1)$ and $B(2,3,5)$.
S: **Direction:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(1,2,4)$.
S: **Vector:** $\mathbf r=(1,1,1)+\lambda(1,2,4)$.
S: **Parametric:** $x=1+\lambda$, $y=1+2\lambda$, $z=1+4\lambda$.
S: **Cartesian:** make $\lambda$ the subject of each: $x-1=\frac{y-1}2=\frac{z-1}4$.
A: $\mathbf r=(1,1,1)+\lambda(1,2,4)$; $x-1=\frac{y-1}2=\frac{z-1}4$
@ex Intersecting lines (full working)
Q: $L_1$: $\mathbf r=(1,0,2)+\lambda(1,1,0)$, $L_2$: $\mathbf r=(0,1,1)+\mu(2,0,1)$. Do they intersect?
S: **Equate:** $1+\lambda=2\mu$; $\lambda=1$; $2=1+\mu$.
S: **Solve two:** from the second and third, $\lambda=1$, $\mu=1$.
S: **Test the first:** $1+1=2=2(1)$ ✓.
S: **Point:** $(1,0,2)+1(1,1,0)=(2,1,2)$.
A: They intersect at $(2,1,2)$
@ex Skew lines (full working)
Q: $L_1$: $\mathbf r=(0,0,0)+\lambda(1,0,1)$, $L_2$: $\mathbf r=(0,1,0)+\mu(0,0,1)$. Classify.
S: **Directions:** $(1,0,1)$ and $(0,0,1)$ are not multiples, so not parallel.
S: **Equate:** $x$: $\lambda=0$; $y$: $0=1$.
S: **Contradiction:** $0=1$ is impossible, so no intersection.
S: **Conclude:** not parallel and not intersecting: skew.
A: Skew
@ex Is a point on a line? (full working)
Q: Is $(4,7,13)$ on $\mathbf r=(1,1,1)+\lambda(1,2,4)$?
S: **$x$:** $1+\lambda=4\Rightarrow\lambda=3$.
S: **$y$:** $1+2(3)=7$ ✓.
S: **$z$:** $1+4(3)=13$ ✓.
S: **All agree:** yes, with $\lambda=3$.
A: Yes ($\lambda=3$)
@warn Never conclude "intersecting" from two equations. You must test the third; that is where skew lines are detected.

## 3.6
@p **What this topic is about.** The **cross (vector) product** multiplies two 3D vectors to give a **vector perpendicular to both**. It finds normals to planes, areas of parallelograms and triangles, and (with the dot product) volumes.
@key $$\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf i&\mathbf j&\mathbf k\\a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\end{vmatrix}=(a_2b_3-a_3b_2)\mathbf i-(a_1b_3-a_3b_1)\mathbf j+(a_1b_2-a_2b_1)\mathbf k$$ $|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta$ = area of the parallelogram on $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$. Triangle area $=\frac12|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|$.
@p **Properties.** $\mathbf b\times\mathbf a=-(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)$ (order matters). $\mathbf a\times\mathbf a=\mathbf 0$; parallel vectors have zero cross product. $\mathbf i\times\mathbf j=\mathbf k$, $\mathbf j\times\mathbf k=\mathbf i$, $\mathbf k\times\mathbf i=\mathbf j$ (cyclic). Direction follows the right-hand rule.
@p **Remember the minus sign** on the $\mathbf j$ component. A reliable check: the answer must have dot product 0 with both $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$.
@key **Volume of a parallelepiped:** $|\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)|$. If this is 0, the three vectors are coplanar.
@ex Computing a cross product (full working)
Q: Find $(1,2,3)\times(4,5,6)$.
S: **$\mathbf i$:** $2\times6-3\times5=12-15=-3$.
S: **$\mathbf j$:** $-(1\times6-3\times4)=-(6-12)=6$.
S: **$\mathbf k$:** $1\times5-2\times4=5-8=-3$.
S: **Result:** $(-3,6,-3)$.
S: **Check:** $(-3,6,-3)\cdot(1,2,3)=-3+12-9=0$ ✓; $\cdot(4,5,6)=-12+30-18=0$ ✓.
A: $(-3,6,-3)$
@ex Triangle area (full working)
Q: Find the area of triangle $A(1,0,0)$, $B(0,2,0)$, $C(0,0,3)$.
S: **Edge vectors:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(-1,2,0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=(-1,0,3)$.
S: **Cross:** $\mathbf i$: $6-0=6$; $\mathbf j$: $-(-3-0)=3$; $\mathbf k$: $0+2=2$. So $(6,3,2)$.
S: **Magnitude:** $\sqrt{36+9+4}=7$.
S: **Area:** $\frac12\times7=3.5$.
A: $3.5$ square units
@ex Unit normal (full working)
Q: Find a unit vector perpendicular to $(1,1,0)$ and $(0,1,1)$.
S: **Cross:** $\mathbf i$: $1-0=1$; $\mathbf j$: $-(1-0)=-1$; $\mathbf k$: $1-0=1$. So $(1,-1,1)$.
S: **Magnitude:** $\sqrt3$.
S: **Unit:** $\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,-1,1)$ (or its negative).
A: $\pm\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,-1,1)$
@ex Volume (full working)
Q: Find the volume of the parallelepiped with edges $(1,0,0)$, $(0,2,0)$, $(0,0,3)$.
S: **Cross:** $(0,2,0)\times(0,0,3)=(6,0,0)$.
S: **Dot:** $(1,0,0)\cdot(6,0,0)=6$.
S: **Volume:** $|6|=6$ (a $1\times2\times3$ box ✓).
A: $6$ cubic units

## 3.7
@p **What this topic is about.** Equations of planes and distance problems: point to plane, the angle between a line and a plane, and where a line meets a plane. These combine the dot and cross products.
@key **Plane** with normal $\mathbf n=(a,b,c)$ through point $\mathbf r_0$: $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r_0$, i.e. $ax+by+cz=d$ with $d=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r_0$. **Distance** from $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ to $ax+by+cz=d$: $$D=\frac{|ax_0+by_0+cz_0-d|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}$$
@p **Plane through three points.** (1) Form two edge vectors. (2) Cross them to get the normal. (3) Use any one of the points to find $d$. (4) Check with another point.
@p **Line meets plane.** Substitute the parametric line into the plane equation, solve for $\lambda$, then substitute back for the point. If you get $0=0$, the line lies in the plane; if you get a contradiction, the line is parallel to the plane.
@p **Angle between line and plane.** If $\mathbf d$ is the line direction and $\mathbf n$ the normal, the angle $\theta$ with the plane satisfies $\sin\theta=\frac{|\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n|}{|\mathbf d||\mathbf n|}$ (sine, because the normal is at $90^\circ$ to the plane).
@ex Plane from normal and point (full working)
Q: Find the plane with normal $(2,1,-1)$ through $(1,1,1)$.
S: **Form:** $2x+y-z=d$.
S: **Substitute the point:** $d=2+1-1=2$.
S: **Equation:** $2x+y-z=2$.
A: $2x+y-z=2$
@ex Plane through three points (full working)
Q: Find the plane through $A(1,0,0)$, $B(0,2,0)$, $C(0,0,3)$.
S: **Edges:** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(-1,2,0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=(-1,0,3)$.
S: **Normal:** $\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=(6,3,2)$.
S: **$d$ from $A$:** $6(1)+0+0=6$. Plane: $6x+3y+2z=6$.
S: **Check $B$:** $0+6+0=6$ ✓; $C$: $0+0+6=6$ ✓.
A: $6x+3y+2z=6$
@ex Distance to a plane (full working)
Q: Find the distance from the origin to $2x-y+2z=9$.
S: **Numerator:** $|0-0+0-9|=9$.
S: **Denominator:** $\sqrt{4+1+4}=3$.
S: **Distance:** $\frac93=3$.
A: $3$
@ex Line meets plane (full working)
Q: Where does $\mathbf r=(1,2,3)+\lambda(1,-1,2)$ meet $x+y+z=12$?
S: **Parametric:** $x=1+\lambda$, $y=2-\lambda$, $z=3+2\lambda$.
S: **Substitute:** $(1+\lambda)+(2-\lambda)+(3+2\lambda)=12\Rightarrow6+2\lambda=12$.
S: **Solve:** $\lambda=3$.
S: **Point:** $(4,-1,9)$. Check: $4-1+9=12$ ✓.
A: $(4,-1,9)$
@warn The distance formula needs the plane in the form $ax+by+cz=d$ and the absolute value. Distances are never negative.
@check Plane constant | Plane with normal $(2,1,-1)$ through $(1,1,1)$: find $d$ in $2x+y-z=d$.
A: 2
AD: $2$
SOL: $d=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r_0$. || $=2(1)+1(1)-1(1)$. || $=2+1-1$. || $=2$.
H: Substitute the point. || $2+1-1$. || 2.
@check Origin plane | Normal $(1,2,3)$ through the origin: find $d$ in $x+2y+3z=d$.
A: 0
AD: $0$
SOL: Substitute $(0,0,0)$. || $0+0+0$. || $d=0$. || Planes through the origin have $d=0$.
H: Use the origin. || All zeros. || 0.
@check Distance | Distance from the origin to $2x-y+2z=9$.
A: 3
AD: $3$
SOL: $|0-9|=9$. || $\sqrt{4+1+4}=3$. || $D=\frac93$. || $=3$.
H: Formula. || Normal magnitude. || Divide.
@check Intersection | Find $\lambda$ where $\mathbf r=(1,2,3)+\lambda(1,-1,2)$ meets $x+y+z=12$.
A: 3
ALT: λ=3
AD: $\lambda=3$
SOL: Sum of coordinates: $(1+\lambda)+(2-\lambda)+(3+2\lambda)$. || $=6+2\lambda$. || $6+2\lambda=12$. || $\lambda=3$.
H: Substitute parametric form. || Simplify. || Solve.
@check Point distance | Distance from $(1,1,1)$ to $x+2y+2z=11$.
A: 2
AD: $2$
SOL: $|1+2+2-11|=|-6|=6$. || $\sqrt{1+4+4}=3$. || $D=\frac63$. || $=2$.
H: Substitute the point. || Absolute value. || Divide by $|\mathbf n|$.
## X
@p **Exam checklist.** End minus start for vectors between points. Dot product for angles, perpendicularity and projections (with a unit vector). Cross product for normals and areas (check perpendicularity). Test the third equation when classifying lines. Plane: normal + point. Distance: absolute value over $|\mathbf n|$.
@ex Exam-style: shortest distance from a point to a line (full working)
Q: Find the shortest distance from $P(3,3,0)$ to the line $\mathbf r=(0,0,0)+\lambda(1,1,1)$.
S: **General point:** $Q=(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)$; $\overrightarrow{QP}=(3-\lambda,3-\lambda,-\lambda)$.
S: **Perpendicular condition:** $\overrightarrow{QP}\cdot(1,1,1)=0\Rightarrow6-3\lambda=0\Rightarrow\lambda=2$.
S: **Vector:** $\overrightarrow{QP}=(1,1,-2)$.
S: **Distance:** $\sqrt{1+1+4}=\sqrt6$.
A: $\sqrt6$
