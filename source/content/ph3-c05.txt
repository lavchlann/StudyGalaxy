## 5.1 | Charge and Coulomb's law
@p There are two kinds of charge. **Like charges repel and unlike charges attract.** Charge is measured in coulombs; the elementary charge is $1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$ (the data book lists the electron's charge as $e=-1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$). **Coulomb's law** gives the force between two point charges, with $k=9\times10^9\ \text{N m}^2\text{C}^{-2}$ (the value in the QCAA data book).
@key $$F=\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$$
@ex Two charges
Q: Charges $+3.0\ \mu\text{C}$ and $-2.0\ \mu\text{C}$ are $0.20\ \text{m}$ apart. Find the force between them.
S: $F=\frac{(9\times10^9)(3.0\times10^{-6})(2.0\times10^{-6})}{0.20^2}=1.35\ \text{N}$.
S: Opposite charges, so it is attractive.
A: $1.35\ \text{N}$ attractive.
@warn Use the **magnitudes** of the charges to find the size of the force, then decide the direction from the signs. Convert $\mu\text{C}$ to C ($\times10^{-6}$) before substituting.

## 5.2 | Electric field strength
@p The **electric field strength** at a point is the force per unit **positive** charge: $E=\dfrac{F}{q}$ (units $\text{N C}^{-1}=\text{V m}^{-1}$). For a point charge $Q$: $E=\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$. The field points **away from positive** charges and **toward negative** charges. Field lines show direction; closer lines mean a stronger field.
@ex Point charge
Q: Find the field strength $0.50\ \text{m}$ from a $+4.0\ \mu\text{C}$ point charge.
S: $E=\frac{(9\times10^9)(4.0\times10^{-6})}{0.50^2}=1.44\times10^5\ \text{N C}^{-1}$.
A: $1.44\times10^5\ \text{N C}^{-1}$ directed away from the charge.
@p **Superposition.** The total field is the **vector sum** of the fields from each charge. Between two equal like charges the fields cancel at the midpoint.
@warn A **negative** charge in a field feels a force **opposite** to the field direction.

## 5.3 | Uniform fields and charged particles
@p Between two parallel plates with potential difference $V$ and separation $d$, the field is **uniform**: $E=\dfrac Vd$. A charge $q$ feels force $F=qE$ and acceleration $a=\dfrac{qE}{m}$. Starting from rest, work done by the field becomes kinetic energy: $qV=\tfrac12mv^2$.
@key $$E=\frac Vd,\qquad F=qE,\qquad qV=\tfrac12mv^2$$
@ex Electron in a field
Q: An electron is in a uniform field of $2.5\times10^4\ \text{V m}^{-1}$. Find its force and acceleration.
S: $F=qE=(1.60\times10^{-19})(2.5\times10^4)=4.0\times10^{-15}\ \text{N}$.
S: $a=\frac Fm=\frac{4.0\times10^{-15}}{9.11\times10^{-31}}=4.4\times10^{15}\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
A: $4.0\times10^{-15}\ \text{N}$ and $4.4\times10^{15}\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.
@ex Speed from potential
Q: An electron accelerates from rest through $500\ \text{V}$. Find its final speed.
S: $v=\sqrt{\frac{2qV}{m}}=\sqrt{\frac{2(1.60\times10^{-19})(500)}{9.11\times10^{-31}}}=1.33\times10^7\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
A: $1.33\times10^7\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
@p **Electron-volt.** An electron accelerated through $1\ \text{V}$ gains $1\ \text{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$. A proton accelerated through $1000\ \text{V}$ gains $1000\ \text{eV}$.

## X | Exam toolkit
@p **Gravity versus electric force.** For an electron the electric force is enormously larger than its weight, so ignore gravity unless a particle is stationary (such as an oil drop held in balance). When stationary, $qE=mg$.
@p **Direction answers.** Describe field directions with words such as "toward the negative plate" and always state the sign of the charge when explaining which way a force acts.
