## 3.1
@p **Why there is an acceleration at constant speed.** Acceleration is the rate of change of **velocity**, a vector. In a circle the direction keeps changing, so the velocity changes even though its size does not. The change always points toward the centre, giving centripetal acceleration $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}$. Period $T$ is the time for one revolution and frequency $f=\frac1T$.
@ex Worded problem: spin cycle
Q: A washing machine drum of radius 0.25 m spins at 1200 revolutions per minute. Find the speed of a sock on the wall and its centripetal acceleration.
S: $f=\frac{1200}{60}=20$ Hz, so $T=0.05$ s. $v=\frac{2\pi(0.25)}{0.05}\approx31.4$ m/s.
S: $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{31.4^2}{0.25}\approx3950\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ (about 400 g).
A: About $31$ m/s and $3.9\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ toward the centre.
@fc def | Speed of an object in uniform circular motion. || $v=\frac{2\pi r}{T}$
@fc def | Centripetal acceleration formulas. || $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}$, directed toward the centre.
@fc def | Direction of velocity in circular motion. || Tangent to the circle, perpendicular to the radius.
## 3.2
@p **Identify the real force.** The centripetal force is always supplied by something real: tension for a ball on a string, friction for a car on a flat curve, gravity for a satellite, the normal force for a rider in a rotor, or a combination. In your answer, name that force. Writing "the centripetal force keeps it moving in a circle" without naming it does not earn the mark.
@ex Worded problem: maximum speed on a flat curve
Q: A 1200 kg car rounds a flat curve of radius 50 m. The coefficient of static friction between tyres and road is 0.70. Find the maximum speed without skidding.
S: Friction supplies the centripetal force: $\mu_smg=\frac{mv^2}{r}$, so $v=\sqrt{\mu_sgr}$ (mass cancels).
S: $v=\sqrt{0.70\times9.8\times50}\approx18.5$ m/s (about 67 km/h).
A: About $18.5$ m/s.
@fc def | Centripetal force formula. || $F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{4\pi^2mr}{T^2}$, the net force toward the centre.
@fc word | What supplies the centripetal force for a car on a flat curve? || Static friction between the tyres and the road, pointing toward the centre.
@fc word | Max speed on a flat curve with friction coefficient $\mu$, radius $r$? || $v=\sqrt{\mu gr}$
## 3.3
@p **Design speed of a banked curve.** On a frictionless bank, $N\cos\theta=mg$ vertically and $N\sin\theta=\frac{mv^2}{r}$ horizontally. Dividing gives $\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}$. At this speed no friction is needed. Faster cars tend to slide up the bank (friction acts down the slope); slower cars tend to slide down (friction acts up the slope).
@ex Worded problem: velodrome
Q: A velodrome turn has radius 25 m and is designed for 15 m/s. What banking angle is needed so no friction is required?
S: $\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}=\frac{15^2}{25\times9.8}\approx0.918$.
S: $\theta\approx42.6^\circ$.
A: About $43^\circ$.
@ex Conical pendulum
Q: A 0.20 kg mass on a 0.80 m string moves in a horizontal circle with the string at $30^\circ$ to the vertical. Find the tension and the speed.
S: Vertical: $T\cos30^\circ=mg\Rightarrow T=\frac{0.20\times9.8}{0.866}\approx2.26$ N.
S: Radius $r=0.80\sin30^\circ=0.40$ m. Horizontal: $T\sin30^\circ=\frac{mv^2}{r}\Rightarrow v^2=\frac{2.26\times0.5\times0.40}{0.20}\approx2.26$, so $v\approx1.5$ m/s.
A: $T\approx2.3$ N, $v\approx1.5$ m/s.
@fc def | Banking angle formula (no friction). || $\tan\theta=\dfrac{v^2}{rg}$
@fc word | A car goes faster than the design speed on a banked curve. Which way does friction act? || Down the slope, because the car tends to slide up and out.
## 3.4
@p **Minimum speed at the top.** At the top of a vertical circle, the smallest possible inward force is gravity alone (tension or normal force zero). So $mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}$ gives $v_{\min}=\sqrt{gr}$. Below this the string goes slack or the rider leaves the track. At the bottom the tension or normal force is largest: $T=mg+\frac{mv^2}{r}$, which is why you feel heavier at the bottom of a swing.
@ex Worded problem: roller coaster loop
Q: A roller coaster loop has radius 8.0 m. What is the minimum speed at the top? If a 70 kg rider passes the top at 12 m/s, what normal force does the seat exert?
S: $v_{\min}=\sqrt{9.8\times8.0}\approx8.85$ m/s.
S: At 12 m/s: $N+mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}\Rightarrow N=\frac{70\times144}{8.0}-70\times9.8=1260-686=574$ N (downward, toward the centre).
A: Minimum $8.9$ m/s; seat force $574$ N.
@fc def | Minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle. || $v_{\min}=\sqrt{gr}$ (gravity alone provides the centripetal force).
@fc def | Tension at the bottom of a vertical circle. || $T=mg+\frac{mv^2}{r}$
@fc word | Why do you feel heavier at the bottom of a swing? || The seat must push up with $mg+\frac{mv^2}{r}$, more than your weight, to provide the upward centripetal force.
