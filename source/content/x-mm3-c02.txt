## 2.2
@p **Arc length and sector area.** For a circle of radius $r$ and angle $\theta$ in radians: arc length $s=r\theta$ and sector area $A=\frac12r^2\theta$. These are only this simple in radians, which is the whole reason radians exist.
@ex Sector
Q: A sector has radius $6$ cm and angle $\frac{2\pi}3$. Find the arc length and area.
S: $s=6\cdot\frac{2\pi}3=4\pi$.
S: $A=\frac12\cdot36\cdot\frac{2\pi}3=12\pi$.
A: $4\pi$ cm and $12\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
@key **Symmetry identities:** $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$, $\cos(\pi-\theta)=-\cos\theta$, $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$, $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, $\sin\!\left(\tfrac\pi2-\theta\right)=\cos\theta$.
@ex Using symmetry
Q: Given $\sin\theta=0.3$, write $\sin(\pi+\theta)$ and $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)$.
S: $\sin(\pi+\theta)=-\sin\theta=-0.3$.
S: $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\sin\theta=0.3$.
A: $-0.3$ and $0.3$
## 2.3
@ex Reading an equation from a graph
Q: A sine curve has maximum $5$ at $x=\frac\pi6$, minimum $-1$, and period $\pi$. Find a cosine model.
S: Midline $d=\frac{5+(-1)}2=2$; amplitude $a=3$; $b=\frac{2\pi}\pi=2$.
S: A maximum at $x=\frac\pi6$ means the cosine is shifted right by $\frac\pi6$.
A: $y=3\cos\left(2\left(x-\frac\pi6\right)\right)+2$
@ex Tan equation
Q: Solve $\tan x=-1$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
S: Reference angle $\frac\pi4$; tangent is negative in quadrants 2 and 4.
A: $x=\frac{3\pi}4,\ \frac{7\pi}4$
@p **Equations with a quadratic.** For $2\sin^2x-\sin x=0$, factorise: $\sin x(2\sin x-1)=0$, so $\sin x=0$ or $\sin x=\frac12$. Never divide by $\sin x$, because you lose solutions.
@ex Trig quadratic
Q: Solve $2\sin^2x-\sin x=0$ for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
S: $\sin x=0\Rightarrow x=0,\pi,2\pi$.
S: $\sin x=\frac12\Rightarrow x=\frac\pi6,\frac{5\pi}6$.
A: $x=0,\ \frac\pi6,\ \frac{5\pi}6,\ \pi,\ 2\pi$
## 2.4
@ex Tangent to a sine curve
Q: Find the equation of the tangent to $y=\sin x$ at $x=\frac\pi3$.
S: $y'=\cos x$, gradient $\frac12$; point $\left(\frac\pi3,\frac{\sqrt3}2\right)$.
S: $y-\frac{\sqrt3}2=\frac12\left(x-\frac\pi3\right)$.
A: $y=\frac12x+\frac{\sqrt3}2-\frac\pi6$
@ex Product with trig
Q: Differentiate $y=x\sin x$.
S: Product rule: $1\cdot\sin x+x\cos x$.
A: $\sin x+x\cos x$
@ex Differentiating tan
Q: Differentiate $y=\tan(3x)$.
S: $\frac d{dx}\tan u=u'\sec^2u$ with $u=3x$.
A: $3\sec^2(3x)$
## 2.5
@ex Ferris wheel
Q: A wheel of radius $10$ m has its lowest point $2$ m above ground and turns once every $40$ s. Starting at the lowest point, model the height $h(t)$.
S: Midline $12$, amplitude $10$, $b=\frac{2\pi}{40}=\frac\pi{20}$.
S: Starting at the minimum suggests $-\cos$.
A: $h(t)=12-10\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{20}\right)$
@ex Temperature model
Q: $T=18+6\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}(t-9)\right)$. Find the maximum, when it occurs in the first 24 hours, and the rate of change at $t=9$.
S: Max is $24$ when $\frac\pi{12}(t-9)=\frac\pi2\Rightarrow t=15$.
S: $T'=\frac\pi2\cos\left(\frac\pi{12}(t-9)\right)$; at $t=9$ it is $\frac\pi2\approx1.57$.
A: $24^\circ$C at $t=15$; rising at about $1.57^\circ$C per hour.
## X | Exam toolkit
@p **Radians first.** Check mode before any trig calculation. Unit-circle exact values must be exact; do not leave $0.866$ when $\frac{\sqrt3}2$ is asked.
@p **Solving trig equations, a routine.** (1) Isolate the trig function. (2) Find the reference angle. (3) Use quadrants for all solutions. (4) For $\sin(bx)$, extend the domain to $b$ times its length before solving. (5) Divide by $b$ at the end.
@p **Modelling routine.** Max and min give midline and amplitude; the time between peaks gives the period; a known point (for example a max at $t=0$) selects sine or cosine and the shift. Check by substituting a point.
@ex Mixed problem
Q: Find the maximum value of $f(x)=3\sin x+4\cos x$ by differentiating, on $0\le x\le\frac\pi2$.
S: $f'=3\cos x-4\sin x=0\Rightarrow\tan x=\frac34$, $x\approx0.6435$.
S: $\sin x=\frac35$, $\cos x=\frac45$, so $f=\frac95+\frac{16}5=5$.
A: Maximum value $5$ at $x\approx0.644$.
