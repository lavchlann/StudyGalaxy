## 5.2 | Anti-differentiation of rational functions
@key $$\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+c\ (n\neq-1)\qquad\int\frac1x\,dx=\ln|x|+c$$
@p Rewrite roots and fractions as powers first: $\frac1{x^3}=x^{-3}$ and $\sqrt x=x^{1/2}$. Split sums term by term and expand products before integrating. **Always include $+c$.**
@ex Integrating powers
Q: Find $\displaystyle\int\left(4x^3-\frac{2}{x^2}+\sqrt x\right)dx$.
S: Rewrite: $4x^3-2x^{-2}+x^{1/2}$.
S: Integrate each: $x^4+2x^{-1}+\frac23x^{3/2}+c$.
A: $x^4+\dfrac2x+\dfrac23x^{3/2}+c$
@p For $f(ax+b)$ forms: $\int(ax+b)^n\,dx=\frac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)}+c$ and $\int\frac1{ax+b}\,dx=\frac1a\ln|ax+b|+c$.
@warn $\int\frac1x\,dx$ is $\ln|x|$, not $\frac{x^0}{0}$. The power rule fails at $n=-1$.

## 5.3 | Anti-differentiation of exponential functions
@key $$\int e^x\,dx=e^x+c\qquad\int e^{ax+b}\,dx=\frac1ae^{ax+b}+c$$
@p The $\frac1a$ undoes the chain-rule factor of $a$ you would get when differentiating. Check by differentiating your answer.
@ex Integrating exponentials
Q: Find $\int\left(6e^{2x}+e^{-x}\right)dx$.
S: $6\cdot\frac12e^{2x}=3e^{2x}$ and $\frac1{-1}e^{-x}=-e^{-x}$.
A: $3e^{2x}-e^{-x}+c$

## 5.4 | Anti-differentiation of logarithmic functions (via $\frac1x$)
@p The key rule is $\frac{d}{dx}\ln|f(x)|=\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$, so $\int\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\ln|f(x)|+c$. If the numerator is the derivative of the denominator (up to a constant) the answer is a log.
@ex Recognising $\frac{f'}{f}$
Q: Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{2x}{x^2+1}\,dx$.
S: The numerator $2x$ is the derivative of $x^2+1$.
A: $\ln(x^2+1)+c$
@tb rec=1,4,5,8,11,12 ext=13,15,16,19,20 | Exercise 5.4 uses $\int\frac1{ax+b}$ and $\frac{f'}{f}$ forms.

## 5.5 | Anti-differentiation of trigonometric functions
@key $$\int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x+c\qquad\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+c$$
@p With a linear inside: $\int\sin(ax+b)\,dx=-\frac1a\cos(ax+b)+c$ and $\int\cos(ax+b)\,dx=\frac1a\sin(ax+b)+c$.
@ex Integrating trig
Q: Find $\int\left(3\cos2x-\sin\frac x2\right)dx$.
S: $3\cdot\frac12\sin2x=\frac32\sin2x$.
S: $-\left(-2\cos\frac x2\right)=+2\cos\frac x2$.
A: $\frac32\sin2x+2\cos\frac x2+c$
@warn The sign: $\int\sin x=-\cos x$ but $\int\cos x=+\sin x$. Differentiate your answer to be sure.
@tb rec=1,3,6,9,10,12 ext=14,16,17,18,20 | Exercise 5.5 integrates $\sin$ and $\cos$ with linear arguments.

## 5.6 | Further integration and initial conditions
@p An **indefinite integral** has $+c$. An **initial condition** such as $f(0)=3$ fixes $c$. Substitute after integrating.
@p The **definite integral** gives net area: $\int_a^bf'(x)\,dx=f(b)-f(a)$. Use it for change in a quantity and for area under a curve (area below the axis counts as negative, so split at the roots for total area).
@ex Initial condition
Q: $f'(x)=6x^2-4$ and $f(1)=5$. Find $f(x)$.
S: $f(x)=2x^3-4x+c$.
S: $2-4+c=5\Rightarrow c=7$.
A: $f(x)=2x^3-4x+7$
@ex Definite integral
Q: Evaluate $\int_0^{\pi}\sin x\,dx$.
S: $[-\cos x]_0^{\pi}=-\cos\pi+\cos0=1+1=2$.
A: $2$
@tb rec=1,5,6,9,10,11,12 ext=13,15,16,17,19 | Exercise 5.6 uses definite integrals, areas and initial-condition problems.

## 5.7 | Applications: motion and rates
@p If $v=\frac{dx}{dt}$ then $x=\int v\,dt$, and the **displacement** from $t=a$ to $b$ is $\int_a^bv\,dt$. **Distance** travelled needs $\int|v|\,dt$, so split where $v$ changes sign.
@ex Velocity to position
Q: $v=3t^2-6t$, $x(0)=2$. Find $x(t)$ and the displacement over $0\le t\le3$.
S: $x=t^3-3t^2+2$.
S: Displacement: $x(3)-x(0)=(27-27+2)-2=0$.
A: $x=t^3-3t^2+2$; displacement is $0$.
@tb rec=1,3,5,6,9,12 ext=14,16,17,20 | Exercise 5.7 links rate of change to total change, and velocity to position.

ALT: 2x³-2x²+c
AD: $2x^3-2x^2+c$
SOL: $6\cdot\frac{x^3}3-4\cdot\frac{x^2}2=2x^3-2x^2$.

@comet Extension: Area under a curve between roots | Find the exact area enclosed between $y=\sin x$ and the $x$-axis for $0\le x\le2\pi$.
A: 4
ALT: 4 units^2||4 square units
AD: $4$
SOL: $\int_0^{\pi}\sin x\,dx=2$ and the part below the axis has area $2$, so total area is $4$ (not $0$).
H: Where does $\sin x$ cross the axis?||Split the integral at $\pi$.||Take the area, not the signed value.
@comet Extension: Find the function | $f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}+e^{x}$, $f(1)=e$. Find $f(e)$ in exact form.
A: e^e+1
ALT: 1+e^e
AD: $e^{e}+1$
SOL: $f=\ln x+e^x+c$, $0+e+c=e\Rightarrow c=0$. So $f(e)=\ln e+e^e=1+e^e$.
H: Integrate.||Use $f(1)=e$ to get $c$.||Substitute $x=e$.
