## 1.8
@p **What this topic is about.** Putting everything together to model real situations: population growth, radioactive decay, drug concentration, cooling and compound growth. Modelling questions are usually long, multi-part and worth many marks in the exam, so a clear, labelled method matters as much as correct algebra.
@key **Growth and decay model:** $N=N_0e^{kt}$, where $N_0$ is the initial amount ($t=0$) and $k$ is the continuous growth rate ($k>0$ growth, $k<0$ decay). Its rate of change is $\frac{dN}{dt}=kN$.
@key **Half-life and doubling time:** half-life $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln2}{|k|}$ (decay); doubling time $=\dfrac{\ln2}{k}$ (growth). **Limited growth / cooling:** $T=T_\infty+Ae^{-kt}$ approaches the long-term value $T_\infty$.
@p **A five-step modelling routine.** (1) **Define** the variables and units in words ("$t$ is time in hours after 9 am"). (2) **Find the parameters** from the given information: substitute $t=0$ to get the starting value, then a second data point to find $k$. Keep $k$ exact (as a log) until the end. (3) **Answer the question**: substitute for "find the value", set equal and solve for "when", differentiate for "how fast". (4) **Interpret** with units and context. (5) **Comment** on validity if asked: exponential growth cannot continue forever.
@p **Finding $k$ from two facts.** If $N=50$ at $t=0$ and $N=80$ at $t=4$: substituting gives $80=50e^{4k}$. Divide by 50: $e^{4k}=1.6$. Take logs: $4k=\ln1.6$, so $k=\frac14\ln1.6\approx0.1175$. Using the ratio of two values like this always removes $N_0$.
@p **Interpreting the sign of the derivative.** For decay, $\frac{dN}{dt}<0$. When a question asks "at what rate is it decreasing?", give the size (positive number) with the word "decreasing", or give the negative derivative and explain the sign. Both earn marks if clearly communicated.
@ex Worked model: radioactive decay (full working)
Q: A radioactive sample has a half-life of 6 hours and starts at 120 mg. (a) Find $k$ in $A=120e^{-kt}$. (b) How much remains after one day? (c) How fast is it decaying at that time?
S: **(a) Use the half-life:** after 6 hours, $A=60$, so $60=120e^{-6k}$.
S: **Simplify:** $e^{-6k}=\frac12$.
S: **Take logs:** $-6k=\ln\frac12=-\ln2$, so $k=\dfrac{\ln2}{6}\approx0.1155$.
S: **(b) Substitute $t=24$:** $A=120e^{-24k}=120e^{-4\ln2}=120\times2^{-4}=120\times\frac1{16}=7.5$ mg. (One day is four half-lives: $120\to60\to30\to15\to7.5$, a quick check.)
S: **(c) Differentiate:** $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=-kA$.
S: **Substitute $t=24$:** $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=-\dfrac{\ln2}{6}\times7.5\approx-0.866$ mg/h.
S: **Interpret:** after one day the sample is decaying at about 0.87 mg per hour.
A: (a) $k=\frac{\ln2}{6}$; (b) $7.5$ mg; (c) decreasing at about $0.87$ mg/h
@ex Worked model: Newton's law of cooling (full working)
Q: Coffee cools according to $T=22+68e^{-kt}$ (°C, $t$ minutes). After 5 minutes it is 70°C. (a) Find $k$. (b) When does it reach 55°C? (c) How fast is it cooling then? (d) What temperature does it approach?
S: **(a) Substitute the data point** $t=5$, $T=70$: $70=22+68e^{-5k}$.
S: **Isolate the exponential:** $68e^{-5k}=48\Rightarrow e^{-5k}=\frac{48}{68}=\frac{12}{17}$.
S: **Take logs:** $-5k=\ln\frac{12}{17}$, so $k=-\frac15\ln\frac{12}{17}=\frac15\ln\frac{17}{12}\approx0.0697$.
S: **(b) Set $T=55$:** $55=22+68e^{-kt}\Rightarrow e^{-kt}=\frac{33}{68}\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{\ln(68/33)}{k}\approx\dfrac{0.7230}{0.0697}\approx10.4$ minutes.
S: **(c) Differentiate:** $\dfrac{dT}{dt}=-68ke^{-kt}$. Note $68e^{-kt}=T-22$, so $\dfrac{dT}{dt}=-k(T-22)$.
S: **Substitute $T=55$:** $\dfrac{dT}{dt}=-0.0697\times33\approx-2.30$ °C/min, so it is cooling at about 2.3 °C per minute.
S: **(d) Long term:** as $t\to\infty$, $e^{-kt}\to0$, so $T\to22$°C (room temperature, the horizontal asymptote).
A: (a) $k\approx0.0697$; (b) about $10.4$ min; (c) about $2.3$ °C/min; (d) $22$°C
@ex Worked model: finding the model from data (full working)
Q: A town had 8000 people in 2020 and 9200 in 2025. Assuming $P=P_0e^{kt}$ with $t$ in years after 2020, find the model and predict when the population reaches 12 000.
S: **Starting value:** at $t=0$, $P=8000$, so $P_0=8000$.
S: **Second data point:** at $t=5$, $9200=8000e^{5k}\Rightarrow e^{5k}=1.15$.
S: **Solve for $k$:** $5k=\ln1.15\Rightarrow k=\frac15\ln1.15\approx0.02795$.
S: **Model:** $P=8000e^{0.02795t}$.
S: **Set $P=12000$:** $12000=8000e^{kt}\Rightarrow e^{kt}=1.5\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{\ln1.5}{k}\approx\dfrac{0.4055}{0.02795}\approx14.5$.
S: **Interpret:** about 14.5 years after 2020, i.e. during 2034 (mid-2034).
S: **Comment on validity:** this assumes the growth rate stays constant; housing, jobs and services would limit growth, so predictions far ahead are less reliable.
A: $P=8000e^{0.02795t}$; reaches 12 000 in about 2034
@ex Worked model: drug concentration (full working)
Q: A drug amount in the body is $A=80e^{-0.2t}$ mg after $t$ hours. Find (a) the amount after 3 hours, (b) when it falls to 10 mg, (c) when it is decreasing at 4 mg/h.
S: **(a) Substitute $t=3$:** $A=80e^{-0.6}\approx80\times0.5488\approx43.9$ mg.
S: **(b) Set $A=10$:** $80e^{-0.2t}=10\Rightarrow e^{-0.2t}=\frac18$.
S: **Take logs:** $-0.2t=\ln\frac18=-\ln8\Rightarrow t=5\ln8\approx10.4$ hours.
S: **(c) Differentiate:** $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=80\times(-0.2)e^{-0.2t}=-16e^{-0.2t}$.
S: **"Decreasing at 4 mg/h" means $\frac{dA}{dt}=-4$:** $-16e^{-0.2t}=-4\Rightarrow e^{-0.2t}=\frac14$.
S: **Solve:** $-0.2t=-\ln4\Rightarrow t=5\ln4\approx6.93$ hours.
A: (a) $43.9$ mg; (b) $5\ln8\approx10.4$ h; (c) $5\ln4\approx6.93$ h
@warn Do not round $k$ early. Rounding $k=0.0697$ to $0.07$ in the cooling example changes the time in (b) noticeably. Store $k$ exactly (e.g. $\frac15\ln\frac{17}{12}$) or keep it in your calculator memory.
@p **Exam tip: evaluating a model.** Typical marks: one for a limitation tied to the context ("the population cannot grow forever because of limited resources"), one for a suggested improvement ("collect more data points" or "use a model that levels off, such as a limited growth model").

## X
@p **What the exam rewards.** Marks are given for the correct method, accurate answers and clear communication. Write the rule you are using ("by the quotient law", "using $\frac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f'(x)e^{f(x)}$"), show substitutions, and give exact answers unless told to round. On the technology-active paper, still write the equation you are solving before using CAS.
@p **Command words.** *Determine / find / calculate*: show working to a result. *Show that*: you must reach exactly the stated result, so every line must follow from the last; never work backwards from the answer. *Hence*: you must use the previous part. *Evaluate the reasonableness of the model*: comment on accuracy, assumptions and limitations in context.
@p **Checklist for this chapter.** Limits: substitute, simplify ($\frac00$), estimate. First principles: definition, expand, cancel $h$, then $h\to0$. Exponentials: asymptote $y=k$, intercepts, range. Logs: laws, domain, asymptote $x=h$. Solving: isolate then $\ln$; $u=e^x$; combine logs and check the domain. Derivatives: $\frac{d}{dx}e^{f}=f'e^{f}$, $\frac{d}{dx}\ln f=\frac{f'}{f}$. Models: find $N_0$ and $k$, solve, differentiate, interpret.
@ex Exam-style extended response (full working)
Q: A hot metal cools as $T(t)=25+75e^{-kt}$ (°C, $t$ minutes). After 5 minutes it is 55°C. (a) Show that $k=\frac15\ln\frac52$. (b) Find the rate of cooling at $t=5$. (c) Find when the metal is within 1°C of room temperature.
S: **(a) Substitute the data:** $55=25+75e^{-5k}\Rightarrow75e^{-5k}=30\Rightarrow e^{-5k}=\frac{30}{75}=\frac25$.
S: **Take logs:** $-5k=\ln\frac25$, so $k=-\frac15\ln\frac25=\frac15\ln\frac52$ (using $-\ln\frac ab=\ln\frac ba$), as required.
S: **(b) Differentiate:** $T'(t)=-75ke^{-kt}$. At $t=5$, $75e^{-5k}=30$ (from part a), so $T'(5)=-30k=-6\ln\frac52\approx-5.50$.
S: **Interpret:** the metal is cooling at about 5.5 °C per minute at $t=5$.
S: **(c) "Within 1°C of room temperature" means $T=26$:** $25+75e^{-kt}=26\Rightarrow e^{-kt}=\frac1{75}$.
S: **Solve:** $t=\dfrac{\ln75}{k}=\dfrac{\ln75}{\frac15\ln2.5}=\dfrac{5\ln75}{\ln2.5}\approx\dfrac{5\times4.3175}{0.9163}\approx23.6$ minutes.
A: (a) shown; (b) cooling at about $5.5$ °C/min; (c) after about $23.6$ minutes
@ex Exam-style: equation and tangent combined (full working)
Q: Let $f(x)=e^{2x}-3e^x$. (a) Find the exact $x$-intercept. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the stationary point.
S: **(a) Set $f(x)=0$ and factorise:** $e^x(e^x-3)=0$.
S: **Solve each factor:** $e^x=0$ has no solution; $e^x=3$ gives $x=\ln3$. So the $x$-intercept is $(\ln3,0)$.
S: **(b) Differentiate:** $f'(x)=2e^{2x}-3e^x=e^x(2e^x-3)$.
S: **Set to zero:** $e^x\neq0$, so $2e^x=3\Rightarrow e^x=\frac32\Rightarrow x=\ln\frac32$.
S: **Find $y$:** $f\left(\ln\frac32\right)=\left(\frac32\right)^2-3\left(\frac32\right)=\frac94-\frac92=-\frac94$.
A: (a) $(\ln3,0)$; (b) $\left(\ln\frac32,\,-\frac94\right)$
