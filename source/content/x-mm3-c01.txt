## 1.2
@p **One-sided limits.** Write $\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)$ for the value approached from the left and $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)$ from the right. The two-sided limit exists only when they are equal. A graph with a jump, such as $f(x)=\frac{|x|}{x}$ at $0$ (left limit $-1$, right limit $+1$), has no limit there.
@ex A limit that does not exist
Q: Does $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{|x|}{x}$ exist?
S: For $x>0$, $\frac{|x|}x=1$, so the right-hand limit is $1$.
S: For $x<0$, $\frac{|x|}x=-1$, so the left-hand limit is $-1$.
S: The two sides disagree.
A: The limit does not exist.
@ex First principles with a cubic
Q: Use first principles to differentiate $f(x)=x^3$.
S: $\dfrac{(x+h)^3-x^3}{h}=\dfrac{3x^2h+3xh^2+h^3}{h}=3x^2+3xh+h^2$.
S: As $h\to0$, the terms with $h$ vanish.
A: $f'(x)=3x^2$
@p **Continuity and differentiability.** A function is **continuous** at $x=a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$ (you can draw it without lifting your pen). It is **differentiable** at $a$ when the first-principles limit exists, which also needs the left and right gradients to match. A sharp corner, such as $y=|x|$ at $0$, is continuous but not differentiable.
@int tangent | Move the point and shrink the gap $h$: the blue secant gradient closes in on the green tangent gradient. That is the definition of the derivative.
## 1.3
@p **Finding an exponential from information.** Substitute known points into $y=ae^{bx}+k$. A horizontal asymptote gives $k$ directly; a $y$-intercept gives $a+k$; a second point gives $b$.
@ex Exponential through two points
Q: $y=ae^{bx}$ passes through $(0,3)$ and $(1,3e^2)$. Find $a$ and $b$.
S: $x=0$: $a=3$.
S: $x=1$: $3e^{b}=3e^2\Rightarrow b=2$.
A: $y=3e^{2x}$
@p **Index laws you need all the time:** $a^m a^n=a^{m+n}$, $\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$, $(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$, $a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}$, $a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}$. For $e$: $e^xe^y=e^{x+y}$ and $\frac1{e^{x}}=e^{-x}$.
@warn $e^x\cdot e^y=e^{x+y}$, but $e^x+e^y$ cannot be combined. Many "simplify" errors come from treating a sum like a product.
## 1.4
@ex Stationary point of an exponential
Q: Find and classify the stationary point of $y=e^x-2x$.
S: $y'=e^x-2=0\Rightarrow x=\ln2$. Then $y=2-2\ln2\approx0.614$.
S: $y''=e^x>0$ everywhere, so it is a minimum.
A: Minimum at $\left(\ln2,\ 2-2\ln2\right)$.
@p **Rate of change in context.** If $N=N_0e^{kt}$ then $\frac{dN}{dt}=kN$: the rate is proportional to the amount. This single idea underlies population growth, radioactive decay and compound interest, so a statement like "the rate of change is proportional to the size" should immediately suggest an exponential.
## 1.5
@key **Change of base:** $\log_a x=\dfrac{\ln x}{\ln a}$. Use it to evaluate logs on a calculator and to solve $a^x=b$ as $x=\dfrac{\ln b}{\ln a}$.
@ex Solving $a^x=b$
Q: Solve $2^x=7$ to 3 decimal places.
S: Take $\ln$ of both sides: $x\ln2=\ln7$.
S: $x=\frac{\ln7}{\ln2}\approx2.807$.
A: $x\approx2.807$
@p **Domain and range, summary.** $y=e^x$: domain $\mathbb R$, range $(0,\infty)$. $y=\ln x$: domain $(0,\infty)$, range $\mathbb R$. For $y=\ln(ax+b)$ the domain is where $ax+b>0$.
## 1.6
@ex Equation with log bases
Q: Solve $\log_2x+\log_2(x-2)=3$.
S: Combine: $\log_2\big(x(x-2)\big)=3\Rightarrow x(x-2)=2^3=8$.
S: $x^2-2x-8=0\Rightarrow(x-4)(x+2)=0$.
S: $x=-2$ makes $\log_2x$ undefined, so reject.
A: $x=4$
@ex Index form
Q: Solve $\ln(2x-1)=2$.
S: Rewrite in index form: $2x-1=e^2$.
S: $x=\frac{e^2+1}2\approx4.19$.
A: $x=\dfrac{e^2+1}{2}$
@p **Strategy.** (1) Is the unknown in an index? Isolate the exponential, then take $\ln$. (2) Is it inside a log? Combine to one log, convert to index form. (3) Is there an $e^{2x}$ and $e^x$? Substitute $u=e^x$. (4) Always check the domain.
## 1.7
@ex Log of a trig function
Q: Differentiate $y=\ln(\sin x)$ for $0<x<\pi$.
S: $\frac{f'}f=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$.
A: $\cot x$
@ex Tangent to a log curve
Q: Find the tangent to $y=\ln x$ at $x=e$.
S: $y'=\frac1x$, so the gradient is $\frac1e$; the point is $(e,1)$.
S: $y-1=\frac1e(x-e)\Rightarrow y=\frac xe$.
A: $y=\dfrac{x}{e}$ (it passes through the origin)
## 1.8
@ex Cooling model
Q: A drink cools as $T=20+60e^{-0.1t}$ (°C, $t$ in minutes). Find its starting temperature, the long-term temperature, and when it reaches $40^\circ$C.
S: $T(0)=80$. As $t\to\infty$, $T\to20$ (room temperature).
S: $20+60e^{-0.1t}=40\Rightarrow e^{-0.1t}=\frac13\Rightarrow t=10\ln3\approx11.0$ min.
A: $80^\circ$C, $20^\circ$C, and about $11.0$ minutes.
@ex Doubling time
Q: A population doubles every 8 hours. Write it as $P=P_0e^{kt}$ and find $k$.
S: $2P_0=P_0e^{8k}\Rightarrow8k=\ln2$.
A: $k=\frac{\ln2}8\approx0.0866$ per hour
@warn A model is only valid in a sensible domain. Exponential growth cannot continue forever (limited food, space or money). In extended-response questions, comment on this limitation when asked to evaluate a model.
## X | Exam toolkit
@p **What the exam rewards.** Marks are awarded for the correct method, accurate answers, and clear communication. Always write the **rule you use** (for example "chain rule"), show the substitution, and give **exact answers** unless told to round. Use your CAS to check, not to replace working in the non-technology paper.
@p **Command words.** *Determine/find*: show working to a result. *Show that*: you must reach the stated result, so every line must follow; do not work backwards. *Hence*: you must use the previous part. *Evaluate the model*: comment on accuracy and limitations.
@p **CAS reminders.** Set radians when angles use $\pi$. Use `solve`, `d/dx` (or `diff`) and `ln` for natural logs. When a solve returns many solutions, read the domain in the question.
@ex Mixed exam-style problem
Q: A hot metal cools as $T(t)=25+75e^{-kt}$. After 5 minutes it is $55^\circ$C. (a) Find $k$. (b) Find the rate of cooling at $t=5$.
S: (a) $25+75e^{-5k}=55\Rightarrow e^{-5k}=\frac25\Rightarrow k=\frac15\ln\frac52\approx0.183$.
S: (b) $T'(t)=-75ke^{-kt}$. At $t=5$, $75e^{-5k}=30$, so $T'(5)=-30k\approx-5.50$.
A: $k\approx0.183$; cooling at about $5.5^\circ$C per minute.
